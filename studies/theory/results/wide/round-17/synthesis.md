## account-1

RL-Zero-Math changes the base by 0.24 % of its weight norm, and what carries the effect is a paired low-rank part: the top 8 singular input-output pairs per matrix (about 12 % of the change's squared norm), added to the base at 1.5x, match RL on new samples (hard328 200.25 vs 200.0 of 328 per sample, vote 216.25 vs 215.92, 0.88 of RL's per-sample lead on problems the cued base never solves in 4), while random, shifted-pair, one-sided or Think-SFT edits of the same norm do nothing. Within that part, directions 2-8 (mostly early layers) switch the model into step-by-step deliberation and make each step less likely to slip, so they add right answers where the base finishes wrong (+13.5 points over a second base sample) or never succeeds; direction 1 and the late layers mostly make answers finish (removing the 8 directions doubles cap hits). On-policy RL packs its change into such pairs whatever the reward (top-8 share 0.127 for LM-judge General-RL, 0.135 for Math, 0.030 for Think-SFT), on input directions that the base's step-by-step text already drives, so other fine-tunes share these subspaces (Think-SFT 4.8x chance, Code-RL 5.2x Think-SFT); only a reward's own input-output pairing carries its effect, and that effect stays in its domain (Code's pairs raise code pass rate by +7.0 points, Math's by +1.5). Inference supplies the switch but not the lower slip rate: a three-token 'Okay' cue or a self-opened draw takes the plain base from 224 to 410-411 of 500, level with RL step 100 (418, where the cue still adds 1.7 points because not every step-100 sample opens that way), and T 1.0 instead of 0.6 stops long answers from looping (loop share past 4,096 tokens 0.004 vs 0.52-0.63). Votes stand in for the lower slip rate only on problems the base already solves sometimes, because its wrong answers there scatter (same-problem agreement 0.26; real vs merged wrong answers +0.07 to +0.11 per vote), while a doubled cap or a forced commit finishes its cut answers without making them right. So the untrained base reaches about 78 % of RL's one-sample gain on MATH-500 (410 on the 224-to-463 range), 68 % on hard328 (164 on 75-to-206) and 53-58 % on code, and under a vote of 4 RL stays 8.8 points ahead on hard328 because the base has no right sample on 26 of the 36 problems RL's vote wins.
