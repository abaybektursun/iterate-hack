# Open experiments of round-07

Separation: the log gain in nats of the proposing side over the other side if the proposer's value is measured (normal densities, sd = (high - low) / 3.29, clipped to 3). Gain over best: the predicted count minus the best count of right answers the untrained base has before on the same set, cap and grader.

## A. Telling two mechanisms apart: by separation per GPU minute

### 1. r5-m03-rival:rival:intervention — gain over best -4, separation +0.00 nats, 1 GPU min (0.000 nats per minute)
- Pair r5-m03-rival (theory state: survives; duel: not separated from rival). Proposed by r5-m03-rival-rival: The top-8 edit gives a weak dose of RL's policy on every sample, and top8 plain's quick wrong answers are the plain prompt's own failures. Which problems fail is a property of the problem (its difficulty plus prompt-specific traps), not a flat ~50 % coin flip taken per sample whatever the difficulty
- Run: Keep the usual 4-sample plurality vote over the stored cued base samples, but break vote ties with an independent plain-prompt base sample instead of taking the lowest sample. A tie means no strict plurality among the finished samples' answers, grouped with rln4_lib.agree. The plain sample is the first T3.jsonl row per problem_index (base, plain, T 1.0 / top-p 1.0, cap 16,384), with its answer extracted by grader v3 from its text. If the plain answer agrees with exactly one of the tied answer groups, pick that group. Otherwise, or if the plain row is a cap hit or has an empty extraction, fall back to the lowest-sample tie rule of result-5d032f. Problems that are not tied keep the usual vote. No new generation: the stored rows are reused. Model allenai/Olmo-3-1025-7B (base); set MATH-500; prompt MATH-500 prompt with the three-token RL opening cue prefilled for the 4 voting samples; tie-breaker sample uses the plain prompt with no prefix (T3.jsonl); sampling T 1.0 / top-p 1.0; cap 16384; grader v3; samples 4; seeds the stored seeds of studies/rlneurons/results/day1_3/math500_base_cue-01/rows.jsonl (attempt 0, samples 0-3, job 153825-208b) plus the stored seed of the first row per problem_index of r4/modal_t3-01/t3/T3.jsonl. Count: Right answers over all 500 MATH-500 problems: a problem counts as right if the selected answer group contains a cued attempt-0 sample with score_v3 == 1. Cap-hit cued samples and samples with an empty extraction do not vote. A problem whose 4 cued samples all fail to finish counts as wrong. The plain sample only breaks ties and is never counted on its own.
- Unit: right of 500
- r5-m03-rival-rival predicts 450 [444, 456]: The mechanism says each prompt fails on its own problem-specific traps, not on the cued base's set, so on the roughly 36 problems where the cued vote ties, an independent plain-prompt answer agrees with the right tied group more often than the lowest sample does (about 5 of 36 for the lowest sample). Plain errors still track difficulty, though, so this adds only about 3 to 5 problems over the 446 vote.
- r5-m03-rival predicts 450 [446, 455]: The mechanism treats the plain base as a short-answer register that is right about half the time whatever the difficulty, so on the ~20 of 36 ties that contain a right group the plain sample picks it ~45% of the time (vs 5 of those ties won by the lowest-sample rule), lifting the 446 vote by about 4 to ~450, short of the 453 tie_short rule.
- Best known base-only count: 454 (result-40ed3b: base + cue, T 1.0 / top-p 1.0, cap 16,384, grader v3, vote over 4 after restart, ties to the shortest finished sample (19,697 tokens per problem)); gain over it -4 by r5-m03-rival-rival, -4 by r5-m03-rival
