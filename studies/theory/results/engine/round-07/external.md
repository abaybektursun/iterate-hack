### 2512.12895 — Wait, Wait, Wait... Why Do Reasoning Models Loop?, 2025
- Finding: Every open reasoning model tested loops at low temperature, and looping falls as temperature rises from 0 to 1.0. Loop = some 30-gram appearing 20 or more times. Greedy loop fractions on AIME 24/25: R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B 0.76, 7B 0.49, 32B 0.37; OpenThinker3-1.5B 0.30 against its teacher QwQ-32B 0.04 (Sec. 2, Fig. 2, App. A Table 1). Smaller models loop more, distilled students loop far more than their teachers, and harder problems (lowest accuracy at T 0.8) loop more at T 0 (Sec. 2). Reasoning models loop where their instruct counterparts barely do. Light RL (Phi-4-reasoning -> -plus) leaves looping about unchanged (Sec. 2). Two mechanisms are shown on a synthetic graph task. (1) Risk aversion: when the progress-making action is hard to learn and an easy cyclic action exists, probability shifts to the cyclic one (Sec. 3, Prop. 1). (2) Temporally correlated errors: at near-uniform decision points small preference errors persist across revisits (the top child is the same on 98.7 % of consecutive visits), so low temperature re-picks the same branch (Sec. 4). Temperature removes loops but not the underlying error, so high-T chains stay longer than a perfect learner's ("a stopgap"; Sec. 1, Fig. 1b).
- Where measured: R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B/32B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B, OpenThinker3 1.5B/7B, QwQ-32B, Phi-4-reasoning(-plus), plus instruct models; AIME 2024+2025, 20 samples per (problem, T), T 0-1.0, 30K tokens, default top-k/top-p, no repetition penalty; star-graph Transformers trained from scratch.

### 2604.03616 — The Format Tax, 2026
- Finding: Asking for structured output (JSON, XML, LaTeX, Markdown) lowers accuracy in open-weight models. Most of the loss enters at the format-requesting prompt, not in constrained decoding. Of 39 model-task-format cells with a significant effect, 36 (92 %) are already present under the format prompt alone and only 15 (38 %) involve grammar-constrained decoding. Mean change -3.9 points from the prompt against -1.6 from the decoder (Sec. 1, Fig. 1). Grammar-constrained decoding raises format compliance from 55.7 % to 92.2 % with accuracy unchanged (57.3 vs 55.7, freeform 61.5; App. Table 7). About a quarter of freeform-correct answers flip to wrong under structure (25.9 % with, 24.1 % without the decoder constraint). Few-shot examples and schema descriptions leave most of the loss. Reasoning freely first (a second formatting pass or extended thinking) recovers most of it, and recent closed models show little tax (Sec. 1).

### 2503.20783 — Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective, 2025
- Finding: GRPO's per-response 1/|o| normalisation gives smaller per-token penalties to long wrong answers and larger updates to short right ones, so wrong answers grow longer during training; question-level std normalisation upweights questions that are almost all right or all wrong (Sec. 3.1, Fig. 4). ### 2503.14476 — DAPO: An Open-Source LLM Reinforcement Learning System at Scale, 2025
- Finding: Four training-side mechanisms, ablated one at a time on AIME24 avg@32 (Table 1): naive GRPO 30; + overlong filtering (mask the loss of truncated samples instead of penalising them) 36; + clip-higher (eps_high 0.28 vs eps_low 0.2) 38; + soft overlong punishment (linear penalty over the last 4,096 tokens before a 20,480 cap) 41; + token-level loss 42; + dynamic sampling (drop prompts whose group is all right or all wrong) 50. With the default symmetric clip, a token at p = 0.01 can only rise to 0.012 per update while one at 0.9 can rise freely, so policy entropy collapses and group samples become nearly identical (Sec. 3.1, Fig. 2-3). Under a sample-level loss, tokens in long answers get less weight, and long samples with "gibberish and repetitive words" are under-penalised, so entropy and length grow unhealthily (Sec. 3.3, Fig. 4). Penalising truncated samples by default adds reward noise, because sound reasoning gets punished for its length (Sec. 3.4, Fig. 5).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-32B base, verl, DAPO-Math-17K (answers rewritten to integers so a rule parser can grade them, Sec. 3.5), ±1 reward, evaluation at T 1.0 / top-p 0.7.

### 2505.22617 — The Entropy Mechanism of Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning Language Models, 2025
- Finding: Without entropy intervention, policy entropy collapses early: 73 % of entropy consumption and 76 % of the performance gain happen in the first 200 of 2,400 gradient steps, and over 93 % of both within the first 800 (Sec. 2.3, Fig. 2). Validation accuracy follows R = -a·exp(H) + b, with two coefficients fitted over 200+ points. That puts the ceiling at H = 0 (R = b - a), and the final Qwen2.5 scores are predicted from the first 15 % of steps (RMSE 0.5 % math, 1.9 % code at the last step; Sec. 2.4, Fig. 3-5). The entropy change per step equals the covariance between a token's log-probability and its logit change, which under policy-gradient methods is proportional to advantage. A likely token with positive advantage lowers entropy, a rare one raises it, and the covariance stays mostly positive, so entropy falls monotonically (Abstract, Sec. 3). Clip-Cov / KL-Cov limit updates on high-covariance tokens and keep entropy up (Sec. 4).
- Where measured: 11 base models from 4 families (Qwen2.5 0.5B-32B, Mistral 7B/Nemo/Small-24B, Llama3.2-3B, Llama3.1-8B, DeepSeek-Math-7B); "Zero" RL in veRL with GRPO, REINFORCE++ and PRIME; KL coefficient 0, eps 0.2; MATH500, AIME24, AMC, OlympiadBench, OMNI-MATH, Eurus-2 code, KodCode.

- Finding: In chains of thought, 50.64 % of tokens have entropy below 0.01 and only 20 % exceed 0.672. ### 2511.08567 — The Path Not Taken: RLVR Provably Learns Off the Principals, 2025
- Finding: The apparent sparsity of RLVR weight updates is a bf16 readout of a model-conditioned bias. Under a bf16-aware criterion, SFT leaves 0.6-18.8 % of weights unchanged and RL 36-92 % (e.g. DeepScaleR-1.5B from R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B 53.8 %; Table 1, Sec. 2.1). Raising the learning rate so updates clear one bf16 ULP makes most of the sparsity disappear (Sec. 2.3). The changed positions are the same across five RL runs from one base on different data and algorithms: layer-13 Jaccard 0.55-0.60 against a 0.37-0.47 independent baseline (Table 2), in row stripes in Q/K/V and column stripes in O that appear early and persist (Fig. 2-3). Similar footprints appear in Llama and Mistral. Proposed mechanism: a per-step KL leash plus pretrained geometry steer RL updates away from the top singular directions into low-curvature, spectrum-preserving directions. RL shows little spectral drift and principal-subspace rotation, while SFT targets principal weights and rotates them more (Sec. 3-4, Fig. 1c). Function-preserving orthogonal rotations of the base remove the cross-run overlap (Sec. 1). Updating only principal weights trains worst; updating only non-principal, low-magnitude weights tracks full RL; PiSSA gives no gain over LoRA (Sec. 5).
- Where measured: released checkpoint pairs incl. R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B -> DeepScaleR-1.5B and a 3,000+-step mixed-domain RL run; GRPO, DAPO, REINFORCE++; dense and MoE models, Llama, Mistral; verl with fp32 optimizer states.

### https://aclanthology.org/D19-1331 — On NMT Search Errors and Model Errors: Cat Got Your Tongue?, 2019
- Finding: Outside reasoning, in machine translation, exact search shows that the model's single most probable output is the empty translation for 51.8 % of WMT15 En-De test sentences. Exact search scores BLEU 2.1 with length ratio 0.06, against 30.3 for beam-10 and 29.3 for greedy. The usable systems depend on search errors: 57.7 % (beam-10) and 73.6 % (greedy) of sentences do not reach the global best score (Table 1). Larger beams reduce search errors but shorten outputs and lower BLEU, and beam-100 still has 53.6 % search errors (Fig. 1-2). The empty-best rate rises to almost all sentences longer than 40 tokens (Fig. 4). Other architectures show it too: LSTM 47.7 %, SliceNet 41.2 %, Transformer-Big 25.8 % (Table 2). Forbidding outputs shorter than 0.25x the source still leaves a peak of too-short outputs, so the bias toward short hypotheses is in the model. Length normalisation fixes length but exact search under it still trails beam-10 (36.4 vs 37.0 BLEU; Sec. 4, Tables 3-4).
- Where measured: Transformer-base trained with Tensor2Tensor on WMT18 En-De (no ParaCrawl), WMT news-test2015 (2,169 sentences), exact DFS decoding with admissible pruning; length-constrained runs on 48.3-73.0 % subsets.

### 1706.04599 — On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks, 2017
- Finding: Outside language models, deeper and wider networks with BatchNorm and less weight decay are overconfident. After the training set is nearly all classified correctly, NLL keeps falling by raising confidence, so test NLL overfits while test error still improves (CIFAR-100 ResNet-110: error 29 % -> 27 % in the region where NLL overfits; Sec. 3, Fig. 3). One scalar temperature fitted on held-out NLL fixes most of it without changing any argmax: ECE (15 bins) CIFAR-100 ResNet-110 16.53 % -> 1.26 %, ImageNet ResNet-152 5.48 % -> 1.86 %, CUB Birds ResNet-50 9.19 % -> 1.85 %, while matrix scaling overfits (25.49 % on CIFAR-100 ResNet-110) (Table 1). Calibration and accuracy are not optimised by the same weight decay (Sec. 3, Fig. 2).
- Where measured: image (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, ImageNet, CUB, Stanford Cars) and document classification (20 News, Reuters, SST) with ResNet, Wide ResNet, DenseNet, LeNet, DAN and TreeLSTM models; post-hoc calibration on a held-out split.
