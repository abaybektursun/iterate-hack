### 2402.05201 — The Effect of Sampling Temperature on Problem Solving in Large Language Models, 2024
- Finding: Accuracy is flat across temperature 0.0-1.0 for chat models on multiple-choice problem solving (Kruskal-Wallis over 11 temperatures, GPT-3.5 CoT prompt H(10)=10.407, p=0.406; baseline prompt p=1.000; Sec 3.1); above 1.0 accuracy falls from T 1.2 and reaches chance at T 1.4 (Sec 3.1, Fig. 4), while text similarity falls steadily with temperature (Sec 3.2).
- Where measured: GPT-3.5 on a 1,000-question MCQA exam (100 each from ARC, AQUA-RAT, LogiQA, LSAT, MedMCQA, SAT etc.), 10 runs per question; GPT-4 and Llama 2 7B/70B on a 100-question exam (Llama results at chance, excluded).

### 1904.09751 — The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration, 2020
- Finding: Maximization-based decoding produces text far more probable than human text and falls into repetition: share of generations ending in repetition is 73.66 % for greedy and 28.94 % for beam 16, against 0.28 % for human text, 0.22 % for pure sampling, 0.66 % at T 0.9, 8.86 % for top-k 40 at T 0.7 and 0.36 % for nucleus p 0.95 (Table 1). The probability of a repeated phrase rises with each repetition, a positive feedback loop that held for most phrases tested, including random ones (Sec 3, Fig. 4). Pure sampling instead drifts into incoherence (perplexity 22.73 vs human 12.38) by sampling too many tail tokens (Sec 4.2); human text does not stay in high-probability zones for consecutive steps (Sec 4.3).
- Where measured: GPT-2 Large (762M), 5,000 continuations of WebText held-out prompts, up to 200 tokens.

### 2206.02369 — Learning to Break the Loop: Analyzing and Mitigating Repetitions for Neural Text Generation, 2022
- Finding: Sentence-level repetition is self-reinforcing: after one prior copy of a sentence, more than 90 % of its tokens get higher probability than on first occurrence (IP_1 > 90 %, all corpora; Sec 2.2, Fig. 3b), and token probability, increased-probability rate and argmax "winner rate" rise monotonically with the number of repeats before saturating. Sentences with higher initial probability reinforce faster, and maximization-based decoding produces high-probability sentences, so greedy-like decoding enters loops; stochastic sampling lowers the starting likelihood and avoids argmax lock-in (Sec 2.2 Analyses). Training on pseudo-repeated data with a penalty (DITTO) cuts repetition without perplexity loss (Sec 3-4).
- Where measured: 16-layer Transformer LM trained on Wikitext-103; 1,000 sentences each from random tokens, BookCorpus and Wikitext-103 dev, each repeated N=100 times; generation on Wikitext-103 and CNN/DailyMail.

### 2407.01082 — Turning Up the Heat: Min-p Sampling for Creative and Coherent LLM Outputs, 2024
- Finding: Truncating at a threshold proportional to the top token's probability (p_base x p_max) keeps accuracy at high temperature where pure temperature and top-p collapse: GSM8K 8-shot CoT at T 1.5, min-p 18.42 % vs top-p 0.68 % and temperature-only 0.00 %; at T 1.0, 30.86 vs 27.67 vs 17.51; at T 0.7 top-p 36.09 vs min-p 35.18 (Table 2). Below T ~1.5 min-p and top-p are within error (Sec 5.2.2, Table 10); stacking several truncation rules was worse than one (Sec 3.4, Table 12).
- Where measured: Mistral-7B (also Llama 3 1B-70B, Mistral Large 123B), GPQA Main 5-shot and GSM8K CoT 8-shot, vLLM; min-p base 0.05/0.1, top-p 0.9, T 0.7-3.0.

### 2501.18585 — Thoughts Are All Over the Place: On the Underthinking of o1-Like LLMs, 2025
- Finding: Long-CoT models switch "thoughts" more often on harder problems and on wrong answers, using 225 % more tokens on incorrect than correct responses with 418 % more thought switches (Sec 1, Fig. 1); over 70 % of incorrect responses contain at least one correct thought that was abandoned, often an early one (Sec 2.2, Figs. 5-6). A decoding-time logit penalty on thought-switch tokens ("alternatively" etc.) for 600 tokens after a thought starts raises QwQ-32B-Preview AIME2024 pass@1 38.3 -> 44.1 and pass@16 62.7 -> 74.0, R1-Distill-Qwen-32B 61.4 -> 64.1, with smaller gains on MATH500 level 5 (83.1 -> 83.7) and GPQA (57.6 -> 59.1) (Sec 3.2, Table 3).
- Where measured: QwQ-32B-Preview, DeepSeek-R1-671B, DeepSeek-R1-Preview, R1-Distill-Qwen-32B; MATH500 (level 5), GPQA Diamond, AIME 2022-2024; 32 samples at T 0.7, top-p 0.95; thoughts segmented by Llama-3.3-70B and judged by R1-Distill models.

### 2502.07266 — When More is Less: Understanding Chain-of-Thought Length in LLMs, 2025
- Finding: Accuracy follows an inverted U in CoT length: the accuracy-maximizing number of reasoning steps falls with model capability (14 steps for Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct vs 4 for 72B on MATH level 5) and rises with problem difficulty (correlation p = 1e-8 for the 1.5B model) (Sec 2.1, Fig. 2a-b); the gap between optimal-length and longest CoTs reaches ~40 accuracy points for the 72B model (Fig. 2c). Under GRPO the mean response length can fall as accuracy rises, leaving the RL model with shorter CoTs than its start (Sec 2.2, Fig. 1b). A synthetic arithmetic study and theory attribute the optimum to error accumulation over more steps vs harder individual steps (Secs 3-4).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-Instruct 1.5B-72B, 60 length-varied solutions per question on 30 (size study) or 100 (difficulty study) MATH level-5 questions; GRPO on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with LeetCode-2K; 6-layer GPT-2 on synthetic addition chains.

### 2509.09677 — The Illusion of Diminishing Returns: Measuring Long Horizon Execution in LLMs, 2025
- Finding: With plan and knowledge given in context, per-step accuracy still falls as a task gets longer; injecting errors into a model's own prior turns lowers its later-turn accuracy beyond the pure long-context effect ("self-conditioning"; Sec 3.2, Fig. 5), and scaling to 200B+ models removes long-context degradation but not self-conditioning (Result 4). Qwen3 thinking (RL-trained) models no longer self-condition even when the whole prior history is wrong (Fig. 6). Even the best non-thinking model (Qwen3-32B) falls below 50 % task accuracy within 15 turns despite near-perfect first-step accuracy (Sec 3.1); horizon length grows hyperbolically with step accuracy above ~80 % (Sec 2.1).
- Where measured: Qwen3 4B-32B and Gemma3 4B-27B (plus Kimi-K2, DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3-235B) on a running-sum key-value retrieval task with in-context dictionary, K=1 key per turn, up to 100 turns, 100 samples; counterfactual histories with set error rates.

### 2505.22756 — Decomposing Elements of Problem Solving: What "Math" Does RL Teach?, 2025
- Finding: GRPO mainly raises precision on problems the model already partly solves ("temperature distillation"): before GRPO higher temperature lowers precision on easy problems and slightly helps hard ones; after GRPO per-problem precision curves are flat across T 0-1.2 (Sec 3.2, Fig. 2). Coverage (pass@64 at each model's best temperature) does not rise (Sec 3.3). Gains peak for test problems with ~60 % pre-GRPO precision (max ~35 points absolute; training-set peak at 40 % precision, +45 points) and GRPO solves no new test problems (Sec 3.3, Fig. 3). Plan grade is high even at 0.5B; execution grade scales with size and is what GRPO improves; GRPO fixes basic math/logic slips but not advanced-math errors (Sec 4.2-4.3, Figs. 4-5). Solution trees branch on semantically irrelevant wording ("unit" vs "block") (Sec 4.3).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-Instruct 0.5B/1.5B/7B, GRPO on MATH train (SimpleRL-Zoo setup, VERL), MATH-500, 64 samples per problem at T 0-1.2, 5 seeds; plan/execution labels by GPT-4.1-mini against reference-solution step summaries.

### 2507.14843 — The Invisible Leash? Why RLVR May or May Not Escape Its Origin, 2025
- Finding: Matching verified successes question by question at large sampling budgets, RLVR keeps most of the base's solvable questions (support retention 0.93-0.99) while newly solved questions are at most 2 % of RLVR successes (NDR <= 0.02), and more questions lose all verified successes than gain one (e.g. ProRL-1.5B-v1 math: preserved 1,355, expanded 5, shrunk 56; Table 1, Sec 3.2). For OLMo-2-0425-1B (k=256) turnover is larger: 83 expanded vs 104 shrunk of 948 (Table 1). On hard graph coloring ProRL is right on 96 % in one attempt vs 12 % for the base, and both reach 100 % at 2,048 attempts (Sec 3.2, Fig. 2). Some RLVR models raise average token entropy while lowering final-answer entropy (Sec 1, Sec 4).
- Where measured: ProRL-1.5B v1/v2 vs DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B; Nemotron 7B/14B, Skywork-OR1-7B, Phi4-Reason-Plus-14B, OLMo-2-0425-1B, Kangheng-OVR-7B vs their starting models; MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench, AIME24/25, AMC23 plus SimpleQA, LiveBench, SciBench, LiveCodeBench, Reasoning Gym; k = 4,096-8,192 for math (256 for OLMo).

### 2505.24864 — ProRL: Prolonged Reinforcement Learning Expands Reasoning Boundaries in Large Language Models, 2025
- Finding: The change in pass@128 after long RL is negatively correlated with the base's own pass@128 per task: tasks the base already covers show flat or lower pass@128 after RL (sharpening, "diminish"), especially in math, while tasks where the base is weak gain both pass@1 and pass@128 (Sec 4.1-4.2, Figs. 3-4); tasks with small gains have base outputs with lower creativity index against Dolma, i.e. more overlap with pretraining text (Sec 4.1). Pass@1 math average 44.45 -> 60.14 over the starting model, e.g. AIME24 28.54 -> 48.13, MATH 82.90 -> 91.89 (Table 1); Reasoning Gym boxnet 0.00 -> 7.91 (Table 3).
- Where measured: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B trained ~16k H100-hours with GRPO + DAPO tricks (eps_high 0.4, dynamic sampling), KL loss and reference resets, rollout T 1.2, 8k-16k context, 136K math/code/STEM/logic/IF problems; eval T 0.6, top-p 0.95, 32k tokens, 16 samples (pass@1) or 256 samples (pass@k).

### 2012.03107 — When Do Curricula Work?, 2021
- Finding: Outside language models, the order in which examples become correctly classified ("learned iteration") is consistent across runs, optimizers and architectures, especially within an architecture family, so example difficulty is largely a property of the data (Sec 2, Fig. 2, Spearman correlation matrix over 142 architecture/optimizer settings); explicit easy-first, hard-first and random orderings give about the same final accuracy in standard training, with curriculum helping only under a short training budget or label noise (Sec 1 contributions, Fig. 1, Secs 4-6). Standard difficulty scores (loss, learned epoch, c-score) are highly correlated with each other (Sec 2-3).
- Where measured: >25,000 image classifiers (FC, VGG, ResNet, WideResNet, DenseNet, EfficientNet; SGD and Adam) on CIFAR10/100, FOOD101, FOOD101N.

### 2504.07912 — Echo Chamber: RL Post-training Amplifies Behaviors Learned in Pretraining, 2025
- Finding: PPO with a 0/1 correctness reward drives generations, within the first epoch, to the output format of a single pretraining source and suppresses the others; the format switch coincides with the largest pass@1 gain, maj@64 rises ~5 points while pass@64 declines late in training (Sec 3.1, Fig. 2). A 10x larger KL coefficient (0.01 vs 0.001) keeps some other-format outputs with similar pass@1 and stable pass@64 (Fig. 3). The winning format is neither always the most frequent nor the most accurate at initialization and changes with scale (150M picks TinyGSM code, 1B picks natural language; Sec 3.2, 3.4). GSM8K-only RL lifts 1B MATH-500 pass@1 by up to ~10 points (e.g. 33.4 -> 43.8, Table 1) with little to no AIME pass@1 gain (Sec 4, App. G.2).
- Where measured: 150M and 1B models pretrained from scratch on FineMath3+, Algebraic-Stack and mixtures of TinyGSM / OpenMathInstruct1 / OpenMathInstruct2; PPO (also GRPO, Expert Iteration) on GSM8K train; eval GSM8K test, MATH-500, AIME 1983-2024; samples at T 0.7 for pass@64.

### 2503.01307 — Cognitive Behaviors that Enable Self-Improving Reasoners, or, Four Habits of Highly Effective STaRs, 2025
- Finding: Under identical PPO on Countdown, Qwen-2.5-3B reaches ~60 % vs Llama-3.2-3B ~30 %; Qwen's base outputs already show more verification, backtracking, subgoal setting and backward chaining, and its gain coincides with a length jump around step 30 (Sec 3.1, 3.3, Fig. 4). Priming Llama with short fine-tuning on traces containing backtracking lets RL match Qwen; traces with the same behaviors but wrong answers work as well as correct ones, while empty or length-matched placeholder CoT priming stays at ~30-35 % (Sec 3.4, Figs. 2, 5, 6). RL amplifies backtracking and verification and suppresses backward chaining and subgoal setting (Sec 3.4, Fig. 3). Continued pretraining on 8.3M tokens of behavior-rich OpenWebMath rewrites also lets Llama match Qwen after RL, while a behavior-poor control does not (Sec 3.5, Fig. 8).
- Where measured: Qwen-2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B (and Llama-3.1-70B for behavior counts), PPO via VERL/TinyZero for 250 steps with 4 samples per prompt on Countdown; behaviors classified by GPT-4o-mini; priming data from Claude-3.5-Sonnet; pretraining-behavior counts over 200,000 OpenWebMath/FineMath documents by Qwen-2.5-32B.

### 2507.10532 — Reasoning or Memorization? Unreliable Results of Reinforcement Learning Due to Data Contamination, 2025
- Finding: Given the first 60 % of a MATH-500 problem, Qwen2.5-Math-7B reproduces the remaining 40 % verbatim 54.6 % of the time and answers 53.6 % of the truncated problems correctly, vs 3.8 % / 2.4 % for Llama3.1-8B; on LiveMathBench 202505 Qwen drops to 0.0 % / 2.0 % (Sec 1). On a freshly generated arithmetic set (RandomCalculation) only correct rewards give steady RLVR gains, random rewards are unstable with no reliable gain, and inverted rewards erode ability; on MATH-500, pre- vs post-RL responses overlap more lexically and token-level KL between pre- and post-RL models is lower than on the clean set, read as retrieval of memorized solutions (Sec 1).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B (and Instruct versions), Llama3.1-8B (and Instruct); MATH-500, AMC, AIME, LiveMathBench 202505, RandomCalculation; partial-prompt completion with greedy decoding without template; RLVR on Qwen2.5-Math-7B.

### 2503.20783 — Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective, 2025
- Finding: GRPO's per-response 1/|o| normalization gives larger updates to short correct answers and smaller penalties to long wrong ones, so response length keeps growing (especially for incorrect responses) after reward plateaus; removing the length and per-question std normalization (Dr. GRPO) stops the growth and shortens incorrect responses at equal accuracy (Sec 3.1-3.2, Fig. 5); the std term up-weights questions that are nearly all-right or all-wrong (Sec 3.1). The same length normalization is in open-source PPO losses (trl, OpenRLHF, verl; Table 2). Prompt template sets the starting policy: Qwen2.5-Math-7B greedy MATH500 69.0 with no template vs 0.0 with the R1 template and 61.6 with 4-shot (Table 1). Base models including DeepSeek-V3-Base already emit "Aha"/"wait" self-reflection, and in R1-Zero more self-reflection is not positively correlated with accuracy (Sec 2.3, App. F).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, DeepSeek-Math-7B, DeepSeek-V3-Base on 500 MATH train questions; RL of Qwen2.5-1.5B / Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with Math-Verify 0/1 reward on MATH train (Oat framework); greedy eval at 3,000 tokens on AIME24, AMC, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench.

### 2503.14476 — DAPO: An Open-Source LLM Reinforcement Learning System at Scale, 2025
- Finding: How RL treats truncated (over-length) samples and the clip range changes the result: from naive GRPO at 30 AIME24 avg@32, masking the loss of truncated samples gives 36, raising the upper clip (eps_high 0.28 vs eps_low 0.2) 38, a length-graded penalty over the last 4,096 tokens before the 20,480 cap 41, token-level instead of sample-level loss averaging 42, and dropping all-right/all-wrong groups 50 (Table 1). Penalizing truncated samples as wrong adds reward noise because sound reasoning is punished for length (Sec 3.4); the symmetric 0.2 clip lets high-probability tokens grow but caps low-probability ones (up-clipped tokens have mean probability < 0.2), producing entropy collapse (Sec 3.1, Figs. 2-3); sample-level averaging under-penalizes gibberish/repetition in long samples and lets entropy and length rise unhealthily (Sec 3.3, Fig. 4).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-32B base, verl, DAPO-Math-17K (integer answers), 16 samples per prompt, max 20,480 tokens; eval AIME 2024 avg@32 at T 1.0, top-p 0.7.

### 2506.01347 — The Surprising Effectiveness of Negative Reinforcement in LLM Reasoning, 2025
- Finding: Splitting RLVR into positive-sample (PSR) and negative-sample (NSR) reinforcement: training only on penalizing wrong samples improves pass@k over the base across k = 1-256 and matches PPO/GRPO pass@1, while PSR alone raises pass@1 but falls below the base for k > 8 through lost diversity (Sec 3.2, Figs. 2-3). On Qwen3-4B non-thinking mode, PSR does not improve the base, while NSR (94.0 pass@1 / 98.0 pass@64) and GRPO (93.9 / 98.2) reach thinking-mode level (94.5 / 97.8) on MATH test (Sec 3.2). Gradient analysis: NSR suppresses sampled wrong tokens and redistributes mass to other candidates in proportion to the model's prior (Sec 4).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-Math-7B (4,096 tokens) and Qwen3-4B (non-thinking, 32,768 tokens) trained on MATH (7,500 problems), verl, 8 rollouts at T 1.0; eval MATH test, AIME 2025, AMC23 with 256 samples (T 0.6, top-p 0.95) or 64 samples (T 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20).

### 2407.10490 — Learning Dynamics of LLM Finetuning, 2025
- Finding: A negative gradient on a softmax output ("squeezing effect") lowers the pushed-down response and moves most of the freed probability mass to the response that was already most likely: high-probability outputs rise and low-probability ones fall, more so when the distribution is peaked and when the penalized response was already unlikely (Sec 3.3, proved for logistic regression in App. E). Off-policy DPO therefore lowers the likelihood of nearly every tracked response, including the preferred one, which the authors connect to models repeating phrases after preference tuning; positive-only SFT instead slightly raises similar responses early and then pushes all non-target responses down as the target saturates (Sec 3.3, 4.1, Figs. 2-4).
- Where measured: Pythia 410M/1B/1.4B/2.8B and Qwen1.5 0.5B/1.8B, SFT and DPO on 5,000 examples from Anthropic-HH and UltraFeedback, log-likelihood tracked on a 500-prompt probe set with rephrased, off-topic and random responses.

### 2507.12638 — Reasoning-Finetuning Repurposes Latent Representations in Base Models, 2025
- Finding: A difference-of-means backtracking direction computed from base Llama-3.1-8B layer-10 activations (taken ~8-13 tokens before backtracking sentences) induces backtracking ("Wait") when added to the distilled reasoning model about as well as a direction from the reasoning model's own activations (cosine between the two ~0.74), but neither direction induces backtracking in the base model (Sec 3.1-3.2, Figs. 2-3). It beats mean-activation, Gaussian-noise, self-amplification, deduction and initializing-step baselines (Sec 3.3, Fig. 4; noise also raises "Wait" somewhat). Through logit lens the layer-10 direction does not project onto "wait"/"but" unembeddings, and as a probe it is densely present and does not cleanly track backtracking (Sec 4).
- Where measured: Llama-3.1-8B (base) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, 300 reasoning traces over 10 prompt categories, backtracking labeled by GPT-4o and by a wait/hmm keyword rate (keyword vs LLM judge F1 ~64 %, Table C.1).

### 2505.22203 — From Accuracy to Robustness: A Study of Rule- and Model-based Verifiers in Mathematical Reasoning, 2025
- Finding: Rule-based math verifiers have >99 % precision but miss correct answers in other formats: average recall 86 % (14 % of correct responses scored wrong), recall as low as 0.78 on Skywork-OR1 (Sec 1, Sec 3.2, Fig. 3); recall falls as the generating model gets stronger (long-CoT R1-Distill-7B/32B ~0.92 vs higher for short-CoT models) and as datasets get harder. Model-based verifiers raise recall (84 -> 92 % on Skywork-OR1) but are hacked during RL by patterns such as empty characters or garbled text; a fine-tuned verifier with higher static accuracy was hacked more (Sec 1, Sec 4-5).
- Where measured: 8,000 responses (1,000 queries each from MATH, DeepScaleR, ORZ-Math, Skywork-OR1; responses from Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/32B), GPT-4o labels (kappa 0.933 vs humans); verifiers: verl, Qwen-Math, HuggingFace Math-Verify; RL on Qwen2.5-7B.

### 2408.02442 — Let Me Speak Freely? A Study on the Impact of Format Restrictions on Performance of Large Language Models, 2024
- Finding: Forcing an answer format changes measured reasoning accuracy: stricter formats lower GSM8K / Last Letter accuracy (JSON-mode < format instructions < natural language then convert; Sec 4.1, Fig. 2); in JSON-mode 100 % of GPT-3.5-Turbo responses put the "answer" key before "reason", turning CoT into direct answering (Sec 4.1). Adding a schema constraint drops Claude-3-Haiku GSM8K from 86.99 to 23.44 in JSON (Table 1). Converting a free natural-language answer to the format afterwards keeps accuracy (Sec 4.1). For classification tasks, constrained formats can raise accuracy by limiting answer choices (Sec 4.1, Fig. 3).
- Where measured: GPT-3.5-Turbo, Claude-3-Haiku, Gemini-1.5-Flash, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-Instruct; GSM8K, Last Letter, Shuffled Objects, DDXPlus, MultiFin, Sports, NI Task 280; 9 prompt variants each; answers extracted by an LLM parser.

### 2504.07086 — A Sober Look at Progress in Language Model Reasoning: Pitfalls and Paths to Reproducibility, 2025
- Finding: Evaluation settings alone move math pass@1 by amounts comparable to method gains: seed-to-seed standard deviation 5-15 points on AIME'24/AMC'23 (Sec 3.2, Fig. 2); temperature 0-1 changes accuracy by up to 15 points and top-p by up to 8, with higher temperature giving higher peak accuracy but more variance (Sec 3.2.2, Figs. 4-7); premature max_new_tokens truncation lowers accuracy, most on long problems (Sec 3.3, Fig. 8); the same model differs by up to 8 points across five GPU clusters, and Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B scored 7.3 vs 11.3 % on AIME'24 on two A100 setups with the same Docker image (Sec 3.4); evaluation framework changes S1.1-7B AIME'24 from 22.2 to 17.7 (Table 2). Re-evaluated under one stack, most RL-on-distilled-model gains are not significant (Sec 4).
- Where measured: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B/7B, DeepScaleR-1.5B, OpenRS1-3, II-1.5B, S1.1-7B, OpenThinker-7B; AIME'24, AMC'23, MATH-500; lighteval + vLLM, T 0.8 / top-p 0.9, 32,768 tokens, 10 seeds (AIME/AMC) or 3 (MATH-500).

### 2108.13264 — Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice, 2021
- Finding: In deep RL outside language models, few-run point estimates are dominated by run-to-run randomness: with 100 runs per algorithm on Atari 100k, the 5-run vs 100-run sample-median difference for SPR (+0.03) is ~36 % of its claimed gain over DrQ(eps) (+0.08) (Sec 3); 95 % CIs on the median need ~50-100 runs to be tight, and two identical 100-run experiments can differ by up to 20 % in median (Sec 3, Fig. 2). Changing the evaluation protocol (max score during training vs final score) changes scores by more than the differences between methods and reverses rankings (CURL vs DER) (Sec 3). Interquartile mean with stratified bootstrap CIs has much smaller uncertainty than the median (Sec 4.3).
- Where measured: DER, OTR, DrQ, CURL, SPR on the 26-game Atari 100k benchmark, 100 independent runs each, 100 evaluation episodes per run; also ALE 200M, Procgen, DeepMind Control.

### 1706.04599 — On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks, 2017
- Finding: Outside language models, higher-capacity networks are overconfident: once training samples are almost all classified correctly, NLL keeps falling by raising confidence, so ECE grows with depth and width, with batch norm and with less weight decay (Sec 3, Fig. 2). ResNet-110 on CIFAR-100 has 16.53 % ECE vs 4.85 % for LeNet-5; dividing the logits by one temperature fitted on a validation set cuts ResNet-110's ECE to 1.26 % without changing any prediction, beating histogram binning (2.66 %), isotonic regression (4.99 %) and matrix scaling (25.49 %) (Table 1).
- Where measured: ResNet, Wide ResNet, DenseNet, LeNet on CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, ImageNet, Birds, Cars; DAN and TreeLSTM on 20 News, Reuters, SST; ECE with 15 bins.
