Unexplained results:
1. The cued base does better at T 1.0 than at T 0.6 (410 vs 354/378 on MATH-500; +13.7 on hard328). It loops at T 0.6, cutting the tail at T 1.0 brings the loops back, and T 1.5 + min-p removes them (result-dadeec, result-e870a1, result-44b4df, result-099916, knowledge-787e53, knowledge-4fb5eb).
2. RL barely notices temperature (-0.2 points), while the base swings 6-14 points. The base/RL gap doubles from 10.6 at T 1.0 to 22.0 at T 0.6 (result-533123, result-bac4fd, knowledge-9e4147).
3. a = 0.25 of the RL weight difference gives most of the gain, then the curve goes flat. a = 1.5 gives word-salad tails. A matched-norm random difference gives only +1.4 (result-4b0d5f, result-8f257f, result-9b6af3).
4. The Code-RL difference is nearly orthogonal to the Math-RL difference (cos 0.014), yet scores 450 with the cue vs 457 for Math-RL, using fewer tokens (result-4b0d5f, result-c9b27e, result-e2482e).
5. A fresh base sample (the random cell) fixes 42 of the cued base's 90 misses. Most of the RL direction's gain is fewer lost problems, not more fixed ones (result-9b6af3, result-5e17a6).
6. Voting helps the base more than RL (+8.2 vs +3.6), yet the base vote stays below one RL sample. Length rules fail, and RL still leads at pass@4 (result-5d032f, result-2c2cae, result-1dedfe).
7. The gap is largest on the hardest and longest problems (hard328 12.8; length quartile Q4 -20.0). On harder sets RL leads the cued base even for Wang's 3.0 pair (result-82b4dc, result-5de032, knowledge-9e4147, knowledge-8a31ad).
8. The RL change is diffuse, not carried by neurons. One constant vector per layer closes 23-31 % of the KL, and the strongest neurons are always-on ones (result-54f212, result-4227c6, result-904bbf, result-156f7b, knowledge-69adda).
9. From RL's prefix the base finishes 32 points more often. Length predicts a wrong base answer better than any lens feature (AUROC 0.831), and short base answers are almost always right (knowledge-1497e8, result-f21d2e, result-5e17a6).
10. The cue matches RL-Zero-Math 3.0 on MATH-500 but trails 3.1 by 10.6 points. The only stated recipe difference is a 16k cap and training truncated rollouts as negatives (knowledge-8a31ad, result-bac4fd).

### 2512.12895 — Wait, Wait, Wait... Why Do Reasoning Models Loop? (2025, v3 2026)
- Finding: Looping comes from "errors in learning". If the progress-making action is hard to learn and a cyclic action is easy, the model's probability of the hard action spreads over many alternatives while the easy cyclic action keeps its mass (Prop. 3.1). Low temperature then picks the cycle. Sec. 2 measurements (n=30-gram appearing ≥20 times, AIME 24/25, 20 samples per T in {0,...,1.0}): looping falls with temperature at every size; Qwen-1.5B loops 76 % vs Qwen-32B 37 % at low T; a distilled student loops 30 % (OpenThinker-3 1.5B) vs its teacher's 4 % (QwQ-32B); hard problems loop 51 % vs easy 13 % at T 0. Temperature is "a stopgap": on the synthetic graph task, chains at T 1 are still 2.74x the length of a perfect learner's (67.4 vs 24.6), with 72.25 % accuracy at equal budget.
- Bears on: result-dadeec, result-e870a1, result-099916, knowledge-9e4147. It explains why the cued base needs T 1.0 and RL does not: RL trained on its own samples has smaller errors on its own distribution, so low T does not trap it. Predictions: (a) the base's T 0.6 loops concentrate on the problems that are hardest at T 1.0 (Q4 by RL length; check the 92 low-ratio cap hits against T 1.0 per-problem accuracy); (b) at T 1.0 the base's right answers on Q4 problems run longer than RL's on the same problems, even though no loops are detected.

### 2310.00297 — Understanding In-Context Learning from Repetitions (2023/24)
- Finding: Self-reinforcement: the probability of producing a sentence rises almost monotonically with how many times it already appears in context. Repeating 'Answer is A' raises P('A') from 0.03 to nearly 1.0 (Fig. 2), across OPT 125M-30B and LLaMA 7B-65B, even for random-token sentences. Adjacent tokens reinforce each other from 0 to 0.4 within a few repeats, and distant ones from 0 to 0.2 (Fig. 4). The effect grows with model size.
- Bears on: knowledge-4fb5eb (about 2,600 repeat-responsive neurons after the first induction layer) and result-dadeec. Loops are self-reinforcing once started, so the effective fix is lowering the entry rate (temperature, RL), not exiting later. Prediction: restarting a cap-hit answer from just before loop onset at the same T 0.6 re-enters a loop far more often than a fresh start would. Restarts also cost twice RL's tokens (knowledge-1497e8 shows own-prefix continuations loop 38/78 vs 32/78 for fresh restarts).

### 2603.12228 — Neural Thickets: Diverse Task Experts Are Dense Around Pretrained Weights (2026)
- Finding: Random Gaussian weight perturbations around a well-pretrained model contain many task specialists. Their density grows with model size (Fig. 3). RandOpt (sample N = 5,000, keep the top K = 50, majority vote) matches PPO/GRPO/ES at equal FLOPs from 0.5B to 8B (Fig. 6). On GSM8K, the 86.7 % accuracy decomposes into 19.0 % format fixes and 12.3 % reasoning fixes, with 0.7 % regression (Fig. 9). A reproduction reports OLMo-3-7B-Instruct MATH-500 at +13.1 over greedy at K = 50.
- Bears on: result-4b0d5f and result-9b6af3 (one matched-norm random difference gives 417, +1.4 [-2.0, 4.8]). This view predicts that some random draws do help, especially through format, and that one draw cannot test that. It conflicts with the team's reading that the RL direction is uniquely specific, unless several random draws are run. See 2611.01262 for the counter-evidence.

### 2605.21468 — Extrapolating LLMs via Rank-1 Trajectories (RELEX, 2026)
- Finding: RLVR weight trajectories are close to rank-1. A rank-1 approximation of the delta captures most of the downstream gain, and its magnitude grows near-linearly with training step. Fitting the rank-1 direction on the first 15-20 % of steps and extrapolating linearly matches or beats full RLVR (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen3-4B-Base, Qwen3-8B-Base), up to 10-20x beyond the observed window. Raw two-point weight extrapolation is 1.2 points worse on MATH, because the SVD step filters optimization noise. Higher rank or non-linear fits add nothing.
- Bears on: result-4b0d5f (a = 0.25 gives 31 of the 47 problems between the cued base at 410 and RL with the cue at 457, i.e. +6.2 of +9.4 points, then flat) and knowledge-c3f1bf/knowledge-0b450e (step 100/400 checkpoints exist). If magnitude is linear in steps, a = 0.25 ≈ the step ~700 checkpoint. Prediction: the real step-700 checkpoint scores near 441 with the cue, so RL's MATH-500 gain saturates early in training, consistent with 2505.22617's 76 % of gain in the first 200 steps. Separately, base + c·rank1(step400 - base) should reach the a = 0.25-1 plateau.

### 2505.19439 — Surrogate Signals from Format and Length: RL for Solving Mathematical Problems without Ground Truth Answers (2025)
- Finding: In GRPO on a 7B base, a reward for format correctness alone tracks the ground-truth reward for the first ~15 steps, accounting for about 85 % of the early improvement (Sec. 4, Fig. 1). It then plateaus "barely showing any improvement even after 100 training steps". Adding a length-based reward lets it match or beat correctness-reward GRPO (40.0 % AIME24).
- Bears on: knowledge-9c5052 (step 100 is at the neuron-change noise floor), result-4b0d5f and result-c9b27e. Most early RL gain is presentation and termination, and that part is domain-general. This predicts the Code-RL delta and the first quarter of the Math-RL delta share it. It also predicts that a format+length reward alone, on any domain, gives an Olmo delta whose cued MATH-500 score is near Code-RL's 450 but whose hard328 score stays near the cued base.

### 2610.00991 — Adapter Thickets: Splitting an RLVR Budget Beats Concentrating It (2026)
- Finding: RLVR raises single-sample accuracy but makes samples share mistakes. At K = 16, voter error correlation rises above the base's by +0.05 to +0.20 in all eight model-suite settings (Table 18). Along training it rises monotonically (0.28 -> 0.46 on Llama-3.1-8B), and the 160-vote majority peaks by step 125, then falls 1.4-7.0 points. On 3 of 4 models the fully trained adapter votes below the untrained base (-4.8, -1.7, -4.1). Splitting the budget across K ≥ 4 disjoint adapters keeps the vote within 0.8 points of the base or above it.
- Bears on: result-5d032f (vote@4 adds 8.2 to the cued base but 3.6 to RL) and result-2c2cae. Correlated RL errors predict exactly this asymmetry. Untested predictions: (a) RL's per-problem error correlation across 4 samples is higher than the cued base's; (b) an intermediate checkpoint (step 100/400) gains more from voting than step 2,800, and may beat it at vote@16 although it loses at one sample. Unlike this paper's models, RL still out-votes the base on MATH-500 (474 vs 446).

### 2505.00127 — Between Underthinking and Overthinking: An Empirical Study of Reasoning Length and Correctness in LLMs (2025)
- Finding: Incorrect responses are much longer than correct ones. On MATH, incorrect averages exceed 6,000 tokens while correct ones average under 3,000 (Table 1), with strong negative length-accuracy correlations. Within one question, accuracy is non-monotonic in length, and correct samples concentrate among the shorter or moderate ones. On problems beyond their ability, models "underthink" and stop short. Preferring shorter responses without labels cuts length while keeping accuracy, mostly by shortening incorrect responses.
- Bears on: result-f21d2e (length AUROC 0.831 for a wrong base answer), result-5e17a6 (263 base answers ≤ 2,000 tokens with only 13 wrong) and result-2c2cae (D(2000) +0.9, R(12000) +2.7). The paper's within-question finding predicts that choosing the shortest finished sample out of 4 (not a fixed cutoff) beats vote@4 on MATH-500, as in 2505.17813, which the team cites. The team's fixed cutoffs fire on too few answers (47/328) to show it. The cross-problem AUROC is mostly problem difficulty, which a within-problem rule removes.
