# External mechanisms for the least-explained results (board cutoff 2026-11-15 02:58)

Unexplained results:
1. The cued base does better at T 1.0 than at T 0.6 (410 vs 354/378 on MATH-500; +13.7 on hard328). It loops at T 0.6, cutting the tail at T 1.0 brings the loops back, and T 1.5 + min-p removes them (result-dadeec, result-e870a1, result-44b4df, result-099916, knowledge-787e53, knowledge-4fb5eb).
2. RL barely notices temperature (-0.2 points), while the base swings 6-14 points. The base/RL gap doubles from 10.6 at T 1.0 to 22.0 at T 0.6 (result-533123, result-bac4fd, knowledge-9e4147).
3. a = 0.25 of the RL weight difference gives most of the gain, then the curve goes flat. a = 1.5 gives word-salad tails. A matched-norm random difference gives only +1.4 (result-4b0d5f, result-8f257f, result-9b6af3).
4. The Code-RL difference is nearly orthogonal to the Math-RL difference (cos 0.014), yet scores 450 with the cue vs 457 for Math-RL, using fewer tokens (result-4b0d5f, result-c9b27e, result-e2482e).
5. A fresh base sample (the random cell) fixes 42 of the cued base's 90 misses. Most of the RL direction's gain is fewer lost problems, not more fixed ones (result-9b6af3, result-5e17a6).
6. Voting helps the base more than RL (+8.2 vs +3.6), yet the base vote stays below one RL sample. Length rules fail, and RL still leads at pass@4 (result-5d032f, result-2c2cae, result-1dedfe).
7. The gap is largest on the hardest and longest problems (hard328 12.8; length quartile Q4 -20.0). On harder sets RL leads the cued base even for Wang's 3.0 pair (result-82b4dc, result-5de032, knowledge-9e4147, knowledge-8a31ad).
8. The RL change is diffuse, not carried by neurons. One constant vector per layer closes 23-31 % of the KL, and the strongest neurons are always-on ones (result-54f212, result-4227c6, result-904bbf, result-156f7b, knowledge-69adda).
9. From RL's prefix the base finishes 32 points more often. Length predicts a wrong base answer better than any lens feature (AUROC 0.831), and short base answers are almost always right (knowledge-1497e8, result-f21d2e, result-5e17a6).
10. The cue matches RL-Zero-Math 3.0 on MATH-500 but trails 3.1 by 10.6 points. The only stated recipe difference is a 16k cap and training truncated rollouts as negatives (knowledge-8a31ad, result-bac4fd).

### 1904.09751 — The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration (2020)
- Finding: Maximization-based decoding degenerates into repetitive loops. Generations from temperature sampling "have more repetition unless very high temperatures are used", and every stochastic method repeats when its parameters over-truncate, "mimicking greedy search" (Sec. 6, Fig. 9). Lowering temperature cuts the unreliable tail but also cuts diversity.
- Bears on: result-dadeec, result-e870a1. This is the base-LM prior behind item 1: a pretrained base sampled at T 0.6 with top-p 0.95 sits in the over-truncated regime where loops appear. The prediction for the team is a monotone curve. Cap hits on the cued base should fall steadily from T 0.6 to T 1.0 (119 -> 98 -> 27 observed at 0.6/0.95, 0.6/1.0 and 1.0/1.0), with an interior accuracy optimum near the temperature where loops stop. A T 0.8 cell would locate it.

### 2402.17762 — Massive Activations in Large Language Models (2024)
- Finding: A handful of activations, up to 100,000x the median, stay nearly constant across inputs and act as indispensable bias terms. In LLaMA2-7B, zeroing 4 of them collapses the model (perplexity explodes), while setting them to their mean over 100 RedPajama sequences leaves perplexity and zero-shot accuracy unchanged (Table 3, "Set to mean"). They concentrate attention on their tokens and so inject a constant bias into attention outputs.
- Bears on: result-4227c6 (44 of the top-100 transplant neurons are on almost everywhere; weight cosine 1.0000 with RL), result-54f212 and knowledge-980cbf (~145x outlier neurons in Olmo-3). Mechanism: a small weight change on a near-constant input adds a near-constant vector, which explains why always-on neurons rank high per unit change and why one constant per layer recovers a quarter to a third of the KL. Prediction: the constant-push vectors align with the output weights of the always-on/massive neurons, and a vector restricted to their span recovers most of the 23-31 %. Caution: knowledge-980cbf found mean replacement is not benign on OLMo, unlike this paper's LLaMA result.

### 2404.16792 — Model Extrapolation Expedites Alignment (ExPO, 2024/25)
- Finding: Amplifying a fine-tuning delta, θ0 + (1+α)Δθ, improves the implicit objective while the first-order approximation holds. Too large an α causes "sharp performance drops and abnormal response length increases (e.g., generating gibberish or failing to terminate)" (Sec. 3, Fig. 4). The optimal α shrinks with training: 8.0, 2.5, 0.5 and 0.3 for models trained 10 %, 20 %, 40 % and 100 % of the steps. Longer training also lets the delta pick up spurious features such as length bias.
- Bears on: result-4b0d5f. The fully trained RL delta (step 2,800) sits in the regime where little extrapolation helps (a = 1.25/1.5 ≤ a = 1), and the a = 1.5 word-salad tails match the paper's failure mode. Prediction no item tests: a delta from an early checkpoint (step_0100 or step_0400, knowledge-0b450e) tolerates and benefits from large a (2-8), possibly reaching the full-RL score from a cheap checkpoint. The a = 1.5 failures should show up as longer, non-terminating answers.

### 2610.20639 — How Much of RLVR's Out-of-Domain Gain Is Answer Format? (2026)
- Finding: Four released 7B SimpleRL base-RL pairs were re-scored with one extractor that reads stated answers wherever they appear. 82.2 % (95 % CI 77.4-87.2) of the out-of-domain multiple-choice gain that harness extractors report is answer format: the gain falls from 22.5 to 4.0 points per comparison. Independent LLM readers give 77 %. Base models state answers in text after "the answer is" or "Answer:" (26.6 % boxed vs 58.3 % for RL), and RL moves them into \boxed{}. In in-domain math, where both box, the format gain is close to 0 (Fig. 3). A boxed-only reward recovers about half of the true reward's MATH-500 gain (cited Shao et al.).
- Bears on: knowledge-499152, knowledge-781efc and knowledge-8229be (every recovering arm adopts RL's format: 'Answer:' 0-4 % vs 39-46 %), result-fdc8da and result-4b0d5f (Code-RL plain 390 vs base plain 224). The cued base still writes 'Answer:' lines, so grader v1's 113 wrong->right flips are exactly this mechanism. Prediction: under v3 the format share of the plain-prompt RL gain (224 -> 463) is small but non-zero; v1's version was the format effect. Of Code-RL's plain gain (+166), a sizeable part may be the box/termination format, which the cue already supplies. That would explain why Code-RL and Math-RL converge only with the cue.

### 2505.24864 — ProRL: Prolonged Reinforcement Learning Expands Reasoning Boundaries in Large Language Models (2025)
- Finding: How much RL expands pass@128 is negatively correlated with the base's pass@128 (Fig. 3). Tasks the base already handles show unchanged or narrowed boundaries (often in math). Tasks where the base struggles gain at every k, including tasks where the base never succeeds and RL reaches 100 % (Fig. 4). Prolonged training (2k+ steps) keeps adding pass@k gains on harder tasks (code, OOD boxnet), while easy tasks plateau early.
- Bears on: result-82b4dc, result-5de032 and knowledge-9e4147 (gap largest in Q4: 4/12 vs 12/12 by RL length; 12.8 points on hard328), knowledge-8a31ad(5) (RL leads by 5-12 on harder sets even where the cue ties on MATH-500), and result-2c2cae (hard246 pass@4: base 157 vs RL 175). Prediction: RL's pass@k lead on hard328 persists or widens up to k = 16-64, while on MATH-500 it narrows toward zero. This also offers a reading of item 10: 3.1's longer training (step 2,800 vs 3.0's ~1,900) would add mainly on harder problems.

### 2512.15274 — Well Begun, Half Done: Reinforcement Learning with Prefix Optimization for LLM Reasoning (2025)
- Finding: Continuing from a fixed prefix (the first 15 % of tokens) taken from a correct answer raises accuracy by up to 20.2 %; a prefix from an incorrect answer lowers it by up to 27.5 % (AIME'24, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B; Fig. 1b). Prefixes from a PPPO-trained model raise a different model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) by up to 9.04 %. Training only on prefix tokens beats standard RLVR (+18.02 % at 26.17 % of the training tokens).
- Bears on: knowledge-1497e8 and result-2c2cae (length rules on the full answer fail). Prediction: a label-free prefix selector that drafts several 15 % prefixes from the cued base and continues the most confident one should beat vote@4 per token. The literature already shows a 32-token prefix-confidence rule (2507.18122, team-cited) beating majority vote at equal compute, so prefix choice, not answer length, is the lever.

### 2610.34044 — A Sign Law for Entropy Drift under Sampler Mismatch in RLVR (2026)
- Finding: When rollouts are drawn with top-k/top-p or T ≠ 1 but the learner uses the plain softmax, a constant advantage shift changes policy entropy at first order. The sign is set by the sampler: positive shifts sharpen under top-p, min-p and T < 1. Masking over-length responses gives net advantage N = +0.30 at 2B (entropy falls); failing them gives N < 0 (entropy rises), and the Mask-minus-Fail drift is -21.3 under truncation vs +0.7 under exact sampling. Under truncated sampling, Fail pushes entropy above 0.5 nats by step 17, and held-out accuracy falls from 45.8 % to 0 by step 50. At 9B, zeroing the net advantage raises accuracy 8.2-8.8 points after ten steps.
- Bears on: item 10 (3.0 masked truncations; 3.1 trained them as negatives) and result-533123 (3.1 is insensitive to T 0.6 vs 1.0). The training-time choice between masking and failing truncated rollouts changes the entropy of the released policy in a predictable direction, not just its length. Prediction, testable on stored text: per-token entropy of RL-Zero-Math 3.0 and 3.1 on the same answers differs in sign according to Olmo's rollout sampler, which is to be read from open-instruct. The sampler setting decides whether 3.1 should be the higher-entropy model.
