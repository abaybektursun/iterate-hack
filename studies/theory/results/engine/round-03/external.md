### 2512.12895 — Wait, Wait, Wait... Why Do Reasoning Models Loop? (2025, v3 2026)
- Finding: Looping comes from "errors in learning". If the progress-making action is hard to learn and a cyclic action is easy, the model's probability of the hard action spreads over many alternatives while the easy cyclic action keeps its mass (Prop. 3.1). Low temperature then picks the cycle. Sec. 2 measurements (n=30-gram appearing ≥20 times, AIME 24/25, 20 samples per T in {0,...,1.0}): looping falls with temperature at every size; Qwen-1.5B loops 76 % vs Qwen-32B 37 % at low T; a distilled student loops 30 % (OpenThinker-3 1.5B) vs its teacher's 4 % (QwQ-32B); hard problems loop 51 % vs easy 13 % at T 0. Temperature is "a stopgap": on the synthetic graph task, chains at T 1 are still 2.74x the length of a perfect learner's (67.4 vs 24.6), with 72.25 % accuracy at equal budget.
- Bears on: result-dadeec, result-e870a1, result-099916, knowledge-9e4147. It explains why the cued base needs T 1.0 and RL does not: RL trained on its own samples has smaller errors on its own distribution, so low T does not trap it. Predictions: (a) the base's T 0.6 loops concentrate on the problems that are hardest at T 1.0 (Q4 by RL length; check the 92 low-ratio cap hits against T 1.0 per-problem accuracy); (b) at T 1.0 the base's right answers on Q4 problems run longer than RL's on the same problems, even though no loops are detected.

### 2609.15476 — Temperature Fragility and the Conditional Benefits of Truncation Sampling (2026)
- Finding: 13 open models on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro at T 0.7/1.0/1.3. Truncation samplers (top-p, min-p, top-nσ) help only when temperature has already collapsed accuracy. ### 2610.24715 — When Do Repetition Loops Lock? Truncation Sampling, Bounded Penalties, and a Blind Spot in DRY (2026)
- Finding: Once a span repeats, the context is deterministic. The chance that a loop survives m more repetitions is therefore a product of per-step continuation probabilities, which teacher forcing can compute (Prop. 1). Pure sampling never "locks" (continuation probability exactly 1). Top-p locks once every loop token's probability exceeds the threshold. Repetition log-odds climb steeply over the first few repeats and reach 6 (Llama-3.2-3B) to 18 (Gemma-2-9B) after 200; for half of the sentences every position clears the top-p threshold after 5-71 repetitions (Fig. 1).
- Bears on: result-44b4df (rule-A loops 0 -> 34 at min-p 0.1 and 12 at top-p 0.95 on the cued base at T 1.0) and knowledge-4fb5eb. Truncation deletes the escape mass that untruncated T 1.0 keeps, so loops that start can no longer end. Prediction: under min-p 0.1 the per-step continuation probability on the base's loop text reaches 1 within a few cycles. At T 1.5 + min-p the surviving mass outside the loop stays above zero. The team can compute this offline from the stored loop answers with one forward pass. The team cited this paper's DRY and top-p tables, not its lock mechanism.

### 1904.09751 — The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration (2020)
- Finding: Maximization-based decoding degenerates into repetitive loops. Generations from temperature sampling "have more repetition unless very high temperatures are used", and every stochastic method repeats when its parameters over-truncate, "mimicking greedy search" (Sec. 6, Fig. 9). Lowering temperature cuts the unreliable tail but also cuts diversity.

### 2310.00297 — Understanding In-Context Learning from Repetitions (2023/24)
- Finding: Self-reinforcement: the probability of producing a sentence rises almost monotonically with how many times it already appears in context. Repeating 'Answer is A' raises P('A') from 0.03 to nearly 1.0 (Fig. 2), across OPT 125M-30B and LLaMA 7B-65B, even for random-token sentences. Adjacent tokens reinforce each other from 0 to 0.4 within a few repeats, and distant ones from 0 to 0.2 (Fig. 4). The effect grows with model size.
- Bears on: knowledge-4fb5eb (about 2,600 repeat-responsive neurons after the first induction layer) and result-dadeec. Loops are self-reinforcing once started, so the effective fix is lowering the entry rate (temperature, RL), not exiting later. Prediction: restarting a cap-hit answer from just before loop onset at the same T 0.6 re-enters a loop far more often than a fresh start would. Restarts also cost twice RL's tokens (knowledge-1497e8 shows own-prefix continuations loop 38/78 vs 32/78 for fresh restarts).

### 2608.01624 — Not the Dimension, the Norm: What Matters in Gradient-Free Weight Perturbation of Language Models (2026)
- Finding: Across 49 model-benchmark cells (0.5B-8B), the subspace a weight perturbation lives in does not matter once per-module norm is matched. Random Gaussian frames and SVD frames tie within 0.4 points (Table 4), and swapping singular values or vectors moves accuracy by at most 1.8 (Table 5). The perturbation norm has a sharp upper edge: survival halves at σ ≈ 0.16 (range 0.06-0.28 across seven models), and above the edge accuracy collapses abruptly, not gradually. At equal norm the top singular directions collapse first. A random frame without per-module scale correction gives about 0 accuracy at every σ.
- Bears on: result-4b0d5f (a = 1.5 gives 34/500 word-salad tails; a = 1.25-1.5 flat) and knowledge-c788b4 (a random edit of matched norm gives most of the gain on a cap-limited model). For search near the base, the norm sets the outcome, not the direction. So the RL direction's +6.2 at a = 0.25 against random +1.4 is a real directional effect beyond what random search gets. The paper also predicts an abrupt collapse at a fixed relative norm. A random-direction arm swept in norm (1x, 2x, 4x the RL delta) should show the cliff earlier than the RL direction, and per-module norm matching (not global) is required for a fair random control.

- Finding: In long-CoT models, the first reasoning step has the largest influence on the final answer: it is the step most similar to the conclusion (Fig. 2). ### 2505.00127 — Between Underthinking and Overthinking: An Empirical Study of Reasoning Length and Correctness in LLMs (2025)
- Finding: Incorrect responses are much longer than correct ones. On MATH, incorrect averages exceed 6,000 tokens while correct ones average under 3,000 (Table 1), with strong negative length-accuracy correlations. Within one question, accuracy is non-monotonic in length, and correct samples concentrate among the shorter or moderate ones. On problems beyond their ability, models "underthink" and stop short. Preferring shorter responses without labels cuts length while keeping accuracy, mostly by shortening incorrect responses.
- Bears on: result-f21d2e (length AUROC 0.831 for a wrong base answer), result-5e17a6 (263 base answers ≤ 2,000 tokens with only 13 wrong) and result-2c2cae (D(2000) +0.9, R(12000) +2.7). The paper's within-question finding predicts that choosing the shortest finished sample out of 4 (not a fixed cutoff) beats vote@4 on MATH-500, as in 2505.17813, which the team cites. The team's fixed cutoffs fire on too few answers (47/328) to show it. The cross-problem AUROC is mostly problem difficulty, which a within-problem rule removes.

### 2501.18585 — Thoughts Are All Over the Place: On the Underthinking of o1-Like LLMs (2025)
- Finding: On MATH500, GPQA and AIME24, o1-like models' incorrect answers use 225 % more tokens than correct ones, driven by 418 % more thought switches (Fig. 1). Conventional LLMs show no length difference between correct and incorrect answers. Many abandoned early thoughts were correct (Fig. 5). It is untested on the cued base; note the team-cited finding that fixed reflective-word rules underperform (2610.24663).

### 2610.34044 — A Sign Law for Entropy Drift under Sampler Mismatch in RLVR (2026)
- Finding: When rollouts are drawn with top-k/top-p or T ≠ 1 but the learner uses the plain softmax, a constant advantage shift changes policy entropy at first order. The sign is set by the sampler: positive shifts sharpen under top-p, min-p and T < 1. Masking over-length responses gives net advantage N = +0.30 at 2B (entropy falls); failing them gives N < 0 (entropy rises), and the Mask-minus-Fail drift is -21.3 under truncation vs +0.7 under exact sampling. Under truncated sampling, Fail pushes entropy above 0.5 nats by step 17, and held-out accuracy falls from 45.8 % to 0 by step 50. At 9B, zeroing the net advantage raises accuracy 8.2-8.8 points after ten steps.
- Bears on: item 10 (3.0 masked truncations; 3.1 trained them as negatives) and result-533123 (3.1 is insensitive to T 0.6 vs 1.0). The training-time choice between masking and failing truncated rollouts changes the entropy of the released policy in a predictable direction, not just its length. Prediction, testable on stored text: per-token entropy of RL-Zero-Math 3.0 and 3.1 on the same answers differs in sign according to Olmo's rollout sampler, which is to be read from open-instruct. The sampler setting decides whether 3.1 should be the higher-entropy model.
