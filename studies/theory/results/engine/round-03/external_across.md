### 2610.24715 — When Do Repetition Loops Lock? Truncation Sampling, Bounded Penalties, and a Blind Spot in DRY (2026)
- Finding: Once a span repeats, the context is deterministic. The chance that a loop survives m more repetitions is therefore a product of per-step continuation probabilities, which teacher forcing can compute (Prop. 1). Pure sampling never "locks" (continuation probability exactly 1). Top-p locks once every loop token's probability exceeds the threshold. Repetition log-odds climb steeply over the first few repeats and reach 6 (Llama-3.2-3B) to 18 (Gemma-2-9B) after 200; for half of the sentences every position clears the top-p threshold after 5-71 repetitions (Fig. 1).
- Bears on: result-44b4df (rule-A loops 0 -> 34 at min-p 0.1 and 12 at top-p 0.95 on the cued base at T 1.0) and knowledge-4fb5eb. Truncation deletes the escape mass that untruncated T 1.0 keeps, so loops that start can no longer end. Prediction: under min-p 0.1 the per-step continuation probability on the base's loop text reaches 1 within a few cycles. At T 1.5 + min-p the surviving mass outside the loop stays above zero. The team can compute this offline from the stored loop answers with one forward pass. The team cited this paper's DRY and top-p tables, not its lock mechanism.

### 2509.06608 — Small Vectors, Big Effects: A Mechanistic Study of RL-Induced Reasoning via Steering Vectors (2025)
- Finding: In Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the RL-trained last-layer steering vector alone closes over 50 % of the base-to-full-RL gap (Sec. 5, Fig. 1). It acts as first-token substitution at the unembedding: the top logit-lens token is "To" (cos 0.37). Prefixing "To" to the prompt lifts the base by 10-11 points, about 75 % of that vector's gain. The penultimate-layer vector promotes process words over "solution", "possibly to deter premature endings", and works through the MLP/unembedding path, not attention patterns. The vectors transfer within a model family.
- Bears on: result-4227c6 (110 of the 472 always-on neurons sit in the last layer), result-54f212 and the cue (worth +38.6 points, knowledge-9e4147). Prediction: much of Olmo's last-layer constant push reproduces the opening cue. A constant-push arm should then add far less on top of the cue than on a plain prompt, mirroring the a = 0.25 plain gain (224 -> 405) vs the cue gain (410 -> 441) in result-4b0d5f.

### 2608.01624 — Not the Dimension, the Norm: What Matters in Gradient-Free Weight Perturbation of Language Models (2026)
- Finding: Across 49 model-benchmark cells (0.5B-8B), the subspace a weight perturbation lives in does not matter once per-module norm is matched. Random Gaussian frames and SVD frames tie within 0.4 points (Table 4), and swapping singular values or vectors moves accuracy by at most 1.8 (Table 5). The perturbation norm has a sharp upper edge: survival halves at σ ≈ 0.16 (range 0.06-0.28 across seven models), and above the edge accuracy collapses abruptly, not gradually. At equal norm the top singular directions collapse first. A random frame without per-module scale correction gives about 0 accuracy at every σ.
- Bears on: result-4b0d5f (a = 1.5 gives 34/500 word-salad tails; a = 1.25-1.5 flat) and knowledge-c788b4 (a random edit of matched norm gives most of the gain on a cap-limited model). For search near the base, the norm sets the outcome, not the direction. So the RL direction's +6.2 at a = 0.25 against random +1.4 is a real directional effect beyond what random search gets. The paper also predicts an abrupt collapse at a fixed relative norm. A random-direction arm swept in norm (1x, 2x, 4x the RL delta) should show the cliff earlier than the RL direction, and per-module norm matching (not global) is required for a fair random control.

### 2506.14965 — Revisiting Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning from a Cross-Domain Perspective (Guru, 2025)

### 2507.14843 — The Invisible Leash: Why RLVR May Not Escape Its Origin (2025)
- Finding: RLVR acts mainly as a conservative reweighting inside the base model's support (Thms. 2.2, 2.5). Empirical support shrinks more than it expands. On Minerva + OlympiadBench, RLVR gains 3 new correct completions but loses 48 the base found; on SimpleQA + SciBench it gains 13 and loses 48 (Tabs. 1-2). Pass@1 rises (ProRL 48.9 -> 65.4, DAPO 43.0 -> 61.3; Tab. 4) while answer-level entropy falls, even where token-level entropy rises.
- Bears on: result-9b6af3 (RL plain fixes 65 and loses 12; the a = 0.5 delta fixes 53 and loses 11, against random's 42 and 35) and result-5d032f. The team's "fewer lost problems" pattern is the precision side of this reweighting. The paper predicts a crossover at large k, but on MATH-500 the team sees RL ahead at pass@4 (482 vs 467) and the team-cited data show no crossover up to k = 256. So for the Olmo 3.1 pair the leash prediction is contradicted at small k; a pass@16-64 base vs RL curve on hard328 would test it.

### 2506.22058 — Lost at the Beginning of Reasoning (2025)
- Finding: In long-CoT models, the first reasoning step has the largest influence on the final answer: it is the step most similar to the conclusion (Fig. 2). When the first step is wrong, final accuracy drops by 40 % on average (43 % on the hardest third, 33 % on the easiest). First-step and final correctness correlate at r = 0.60 (Fig. 3). Larger models recover less often from a bad start than assumed. Pruning bad first steps with a reward model cuts inference cost by up to 70 % at equal accuracy.
- Bears on: knowledge-1497e8 (from RL's prefix the base finishes 48/78 and is right 41/78, vs 23/16 from its own prefix and 38/30 from a fresh restart) and result-5e17a6 (14 misread and 9 wrong-method RL-only problems). Prediction: much shorter RL prefixes (the first step, a few hundred tokens rather than the median 2,500) already carry most of the finish and accuracy gain. Equivalently, the base's T 1.0 failures on the 65 RL-only problems are decided early; result-fdc8da found RL's largest log-prob gaps on cap hits at median relative position 0.19.

### 2507.19766 — UloRL: An Ultra-Long Output Reinforcement Learning Approach (2025)
- Finding: In ultra-long-output RL, DAPO's overlong filtering (masking truncated samples) "leads to a rapid increase in the output length, which is undesirable". Treating overlong samples as wrong (reward 0) performs comparably to DAPO's soft overlong punishment, so the paper adopts it (Sec. 3.5).
- Bears on: knowledge-8a31ad(7) (Olmo 3.1 trains truncated sequences as negatives with a 16k cap, unlike 3.0) and item 10. The cue ties RL 3.0 on MATH-500 but trails 3.1 by 10.6, and on hard328 3.1 has 20 cap hits vs the cued base's 77 (result-82b4dc). Training truncations as failures directly penalizes not finishing inside the cap. Prediction: the cap-hit rate is the main 3.0 vs 3.1 difference. Score RL-Zero-Math 3.0 (first release) at the team's 16,384 cap: it should show more cap hits than 3.1, and the cued base's gap to it should sit mostly in cap-hit problems (knowledge-c3f1bf: the fr-01 job never finished).
