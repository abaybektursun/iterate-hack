### 2510.02611 — On the Role of Temperature Sampling in Test-Time Scaling, 2025
- Finding: Temperature leaves mean single-sample accuracy almost unchanged and only changes which rare hard problems get at least one correct trace. Avg@1,024 (mean per-sample accuracy) is "nearly identical across temperatures" from T 0.1 to 1.2 on all five benchmarks (Sec. 2.3, Fig. 2d). Different temperatures solve different subsets of problems, so sampling at several temperatures raises oracle coverage, not pass@1.
- Where measured: Qwen3-4B (and Qwen3 0.6B-8B for coverage). AIME 2024/2025, MATH500 (134 level-5 problems), LiveCodeBench v6, Hi-ToM. 1,024 samples per question.

### 2512.12895 — Wait, Wait, Wait... Why Do Reasoning Models Loop?, 2025
- Finding: Looping is highest at low temperature and in smaller or distilled students. At T=0 the loop rate (a 30-gram repeated at least 20 times) is 76% for R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 37% for 32B, and 30% for OpenThinker3-1.5B against 4% for its teacher QwQ-32B (Sec. 2, App. A). In these reasoning models accuracy rises with T. Once a loop starts, top-1 probability rises toward 1 (Sec. 5, App. F, Fig. 16). The authors call temperature a stopgap. In a synthetic task at T=1, response length is 108.1 against 24.4 for a perfect learner (Sec. 3.3).
- Where measured: Reasoning models (R1-Distill-Qwen, OpenThinker3, Phi-4-reasoning(-plus)) on AIME 24/25. 20 samples per (problem, model, T), T 0 to 1.0, 30K max tokens, no repetition penalty.

### 2601.05693 — Circular Reasoning: Understanding Self-Reinforcing Loops in Large Reasoning Models, 2026
- Finding: Loop rate falls with temperature but does not reach zero, and numerical loops barely respond. Total LoopBench loop rate at T 0.1 / 0.6 / 1.0 (Tables 1, 6, 7): R1-Distill-Qwen-7B 92.07 / 62.00 / 18.71%; Qwen3-8B 35.57 / 29.29 / 10.71%; Qwen3-8B-base 42.29 / 22.29 / 20.15%. Numerical loops for Qwen3-8B-base: 38.86 / 20.57 / 19.86%. At loop onset the maximum logit jumps and entropy drops toward 0. Hidden states of the repeated token reach cosine near 1.0 across cycles, and attention concentrates on the first tokens and the last 128 (Sec. 3.1, 3.3, Figs. 3-4, 8).
- Where measured: R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/14B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-8B-base on LoopBench and AIME25. Repetition penalty 1.1. Temperature changed together with top-k (5/15/30) and top-p (0.95/0.9/0.9).

### 2407.01082 — Turning Up the Heat: Min-p Sampling for Creative and Coherent LLM Outputs, 2024
- Finding: Min-p truncates below p_base times the top token's probability. It keeps reasoning accuracy at temperatures where top-p and plain temperature collapse, and it is no better than top-p below T 1.5. GSM8K CoT accuracy at T 0.7 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 (Table 2): temperature only 29.56 / 17.51 / 0.00 / 0.00; top-p 36.09 / 27.67 / 0.68 / 0.00; min-p 35.18 / 30.86 / 18.42 / 6.21. GPQA Main at T 3.0: min-p 24.55, top-p 0.46. At T < 1.5 min-p and top-p are within error (Sec. 5, App. Table 10).
- Where measured: Mistral-7B. GPQA Main (5-shot), GSM8K CoT (8-shot). vLLM. Std. error about ±1-1.5%.

### 2505.17813 — Don't Overthink it. Preferring Shorter Thinking Chains for Improved LLM Reasoning, 2025
- Finding: Within one question, shorter finished chains are more often correct, and the effect holds inside each difficulty band. Shortest / random / longest of 20 samples, math average (Table 1): LN-Super-49B 63.4 / 47.7 / 28.9, R1-32B 60.0 / 55.5 / 37.8, R1-670B 83.3 / 78.2 / 58.9. Mean thinking tokens for correct vs incorrect on hard problems (Table 2): LN-Super 12.4k vs 16.8k, R1-670B 23.0k vs 31.7k. Correct R1-32B chains have 95 backtrack keywords against 352 for incorrect ones (Sec. 5.1). A sequential variant cuts each new sample at the longest of the m shortest finished samples so far and still beats majority voting (Sec. 5.3, Fig. 5). The effect is larger for small distilled models: R1-Distill-Qwen-7B shortest 57.8 vs random 41.7 vs longest 22.2 (App. D, Table 7).
- Where measured: LN-Super-49B, R1-32B, QwQ-32B, R1-670B, R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Nemotron-Nano-8B. AIME24/25, HMMT. T 0.7, top-p 0.95, 32,768-token cap. Samples that hit the cap are excluded. No base models tested.

### 2503.20783 — Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective, 2025
- Finding: GRPO has a response-level length bias, because dividing by |o_i| penalises long incorrect responses less per token. It also has a question-level difficulty bias, because dividing by the group reward std upweights all-right or all-wrong questions (Sec. 3.1). The same length normalisation appears in open-source PPO implementations (Table 2). Removing both terms (Dr. GRPO) stops runaway length growth and shortens incorrect responses at similar reward (Fig. 5). DeepSeek-V3-Base already writes self-reflection keywords ("Aha", "wait") on the 500 MATH questions, and in R1-Zero self-reflection is not positively correlated with accuracy (Sec. 2.3, App. F). Their minimal recipe reaches 43.3% on AIME 2024 from a 7B base.
- Where measured: Qwen2.5(-Math), Llama, DeepSeek-V3-Base on MATH and AIME 2024. GRPO vs Dr. GRPO training curves in Fig. 5. Final recipe on Qwen2.5-Math-7B.

### 2505.11711 — Reinforcement Learning Finetunes Small Subnetworks in Large Language Models, 2025
- Finding: RL changes only 5-30% of parameters, yet each update matrix is nearly full rank: 99.2-99.8% of maximum rank (Sec. 3, Table 2, Fig. 3). Per-layer and per-matrix (Q, K, V, O, MLP) sparsity is similar across all layers, and only layer-norm weights get little or no update. Fine-tuning the subnetwork alone recovers test accuracy. Subnetworks from different seeds, data and algorithms overlap more than chance.
- Where measured: 7 RL algorithms, 10 LLMs, including Tulu 8B DPO, Eurus 7B PRIME, Llama-3 8B KTO and DeepSeek-Math 7B GRPO. Released bf16 checkpoints compared with their initial models.

### 2509.04259 — RL's Razor: Why Online Reinforcement Learning Forgets Less, 2025
- Finding: The sparse RL weight updates reported in earlier work came from bfloat16 rounding: small updates fall below the mantissa step and do not change the stored weight. "The same training with float32 resulted in models with identical performance but without any sparsity in their weight updates", and every algorithm gave full-rank weight changes (Sec. 6, sparsity bullet). The paper's main claim is that online RL forgets less than SFT because it stays closer to the base policy in KL on the new task.
- Where measured: The paper's RL vs SFT fine-tuning runs, with a bf16 vs fp32 training ablation (Sec. 6).

### 2510.00553 — On Predictability of Reinforcement Learning Dynamics for Large Language Models, 2025
- Finding: The top singular direction of the RL update carries about 19% of the update (Sec. 2.3), yet rescaled to the full-update norm it keeps the MATH-500 gain (rank-1 relative accuracy about 100% for on-policy DAPO, App. C Tables 7-8). Scaling the rank-1 update by λ from 0.1 to 1.0 raises accuracy quickly, then saturates, peaking near λ≈0.7, slightly below the full norm (Sec. 2.2, Fig. 4b). The sweep stops at λ=1. Low-rank structure also appears in DPO and on-policy-distillation updates.
- Where measured: Qwen3-8B-Base trained with PPO, RLOO, GRPO, Dr.GRPO and DAPO, evaluated on MATH-500.

### 2610.37719 — What a Weight Update Buys: Leverage Across Training Stages, 2026
- Finding: Leverage (ρ_iso) is the Fisher curvature of an update divided by that of a random same-norm direction. RL-Zero deltas have a geometric mean of 708 (range 388-1,508) at relative displacement 6.4e-4 to 1.5e-3. Pretraining windows read about 0.86, SFT about 5.5, and DPO 3,304-6,165 (Sec. 3, Table 1). A sign-flip null with the same magnitudes and random signs gives 26-129% of ρ_iso, and magnitude alignment is 1.1-1.6, so leverage sits in signs and cross-coordinate structure, not in large weights (Table 2). RLVR leverage stays at 480-620 along 13 released OLMo-2-1B checkpoints. GRPO runs stay at 200-320 as they move, while SFT and DPO runs lose leverage (Sec. 3-4, Table 3, Fig. 1). The rlzero_math checkpoint has 58% of coordinates identical to base because of bf16 storage.
- Where measured: Olmo-3-7B base vs five RL-Zero runs (Math, Code, IF, General, Mix), on a 51k-token mixed corpus. OLMo-2-1B RLVR checkpoints. Qwen2.5-0.5B GRPO on GSM8K (150 steps, fp32 master weights, one seed per curve).

### 2610.22438 — Rank Measures of Fine-Tuning Updates Need a Noise Baseline, 2026
- Finding: With model, optimizer and token count fixed, how concentrated an update is depends on fine-tuning itself, not on the objective. Stable rank for Qwen2.5-0.5B: SFT 0.097, wikitext 0.093, code 0.094; real DPO 0.053 against 0.055 for DPO with half its preference pairs swapped. Shuffled-word text gives the most concentrated update (0.042) (Sec. 4.3, Tables 2, 14). Top-16 left-subspace overlap is 0.26-0.32 for the same data with a different seed, 0.20-0.22 for different data, and 0.04 for random.
- Where measured: Own runs on Qwen2.5-0.5B and a 0.36B model; released OLMo 2 stages. RL appears only as a released OLMo 2 stage.

### 2603.22446 — Sparse but Critical: A Token-Level Analysis of Distributional Shifts in RLVR Fine-Tuning of LLMs, 2026
- Finding: RLVR rarely raises tokens the base found unlikely. At high-divergence positions (JS > 0.1), more than 80% (DAPO) and 90% (SimpleRL) of RL top-1 tokens are in the base top-3. Only about 5% had base probability below 0.01, and only about 30% were already base top-1 (Sec. 4.1-4.2, Figs. 9, 10, 22). Divergence is highest at the start of the response and rises again at the end, where formatting and termination happen. The relative weight gap is 2.4x (32B) and 6.9x (7B) smaller for RL than for SFT at equal data and steps: σ 0.00143 and 0.00136 against 0.00347 and 0.00944 (App. A.3, Table 3).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-32B (DAPO, SimpleRL) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B on AIME-style math.

### 2312.01552 — The Unlocking Spell on Base LLMs: Rethinking Alignment via In-Context Learning, 2023
- Finding: Alignment tuning changes few token decisions, and those are concentrated in stylistic tokens at the start of a response. On the chat model's greedy outputs, 77.7% of tokens are the base's top-1 under the same prefix and 92.2% are within the base top-3. Shifted (rank > 3) tokens are 4.8-7.8% (Sec. 2.2, Figs. 2-3). KL between base and aligned next-token distributions falls with position, and the base rank of the aligned token is below 5 after position 5 (Fig. 4). Shifted tokens are mostly discourse markers (Thank, However, Here) and disclaimers.
- Where measured: Llama-2-7b vs -chat (RLHF), Llama-2-7b vs Vicuna-7b-v1.5, Mistral-7b vs -Instruct. 1,000 just-eval-instruct prompts. Base and chat use different templates.

### 2506.01939 — Beyond the 80/20 Rule: High-Entropy Minority Tokens Drive Effective Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning, 2025
- Finding: In chain-of-thought text, 50.64% of tokens have entropy below 0.01 and only 20% exceed 0.672. The high-entropy "forking" tokens are connectors such as wait, however, thus, suppose (Sec. 3). Lowering temperature only at forking tokens hurts much more than lowering it elsewhere (Fig. 3). Applying the RL gradient only to the top-20% entropy tokens matches full-gradient DAPO on MATH500 Acc@16: 32B 94.88 vs 94.36, 14B 93.59 vs 92.23, 8B 89.70 vs 89.24. It beats full-gradient DAPO on AIME for 32B (+7.71 AIME24, +11.04 AIME25) (Sec. 5.3, Table 2). Training only on the lowest-entropy 80% gives a marked decline.
- Where measured: Qwen3-8B entropy statistics on AIME24/25 at T 1.0, over about 10^6 tokens. DAPO on Qwen3 8B/14B/32B base models with DAPO-Math-17K, 16 samples. One run per setting.

### 2510.07364 — Base Models Know How to Reason, Thinking Models Learn When, 2025
- Finding: Steering a base model's activations with 10 category vectors recovers about half of the base-to-RL gap. Categories are read from SAE activations, and steering fires only where the RL model's next token disagrees with the base's (5-12% of generated tokens). MATH500: Qwen2.5-7B base 63.9, hybrid 75.7, Open-Reasoner-Zero-7B 87.6, so 49.7% of the gap (Sec. 3.7, Table 1). Gap recovered on ORZ-1.5B and -32B: full pipeline 77% and 77%, norm-matched random vectors -28% and -13%, random category 20% and 28% (Sec. 3.8, Fig. 4). The RL model runs at every token to decide where to steer (Sec. 3.5-3.6).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5 base vs Open-Reasoner-Zero models (1.5B, 7B, 32B). MATH500 and GSM8K, 2,048-token budget. Base and hybrid greedy, RL model sampled at T 0.6.

### 2504.07986 — SEAL: Steerable Reasoning Calibration of Large Language Models for Free, 2025
- Finding: One steering vector, the mean hidden state at blank-line tokens of "execution" thoughts minus that of "reflection/transition" thoughts (labelled by keywords such as Wait, Alternatively, verify), is added at every blank-line token. It raises accuracy and cuts tokens on MATH-500, greedy: R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B 67.0 → 78.0 at 4,526 → 3,154 tokens; R1-Distill-Qwen-7B 85.8 → 89.4 at 3,389 → 2,661; QwQ-32B-Preview 90.4 → 91.0. On the hard subset QwQ-32B falls 86.6 → 85.5 (Sec. 5.2, Table 1). Keyword labels and GPT-4o labels give 89.4 vs 88.6 (App. B.3, Table 7).
- Where measured: Long-CoT reasoning models (R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B, QwQ-32B-Preview). Vector from 1,000 MATH train problems at one layer (20 for the 1.5B/7B models), α=1.0.

### 2604.01094 — Temporal Dependencies in In-Context Learning: The Role of Induction Heads, 2026
- Finding: Heads with high induction scores carry the "copy the token after the previous occurrence" (+1 lag) bias and ordered recall, and random heads do not. In a 10-shot serial-recall task, ablating 50 induction heads drops Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct's lag +1 probability from 0.98 to 0.28, against 0.90 for 50 random heads. The two curves separate from about 10 ablated heads onward (Sec. 2.5, Fig. 5, Table A4). The same pattern holds in the base models.
- Where measured: Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.1, gemma-2-9b, base and instruct. Induction score from 1,000 repeated tokens. Zero and mean ablation via TransformerLens. 50 prompts.

### 2609.15064 — What Does an LLM Learn from Reinforcement Learning? A Mechanistic Interpretability Perspective with Fixed-SAE Track, 2026
- Finding: Tracked with one fixed SAE, RL first installs an answer format and adds a smaller reasoning gain slowly. Continuing GRPO for 2,000 more steps at constant learning rate raised the wrapper (format) rate 0.108 → 0.983 but lenient accuracy only 0.300 → 0.408. About 86% of total feature amplification happened in the first 50 steps (Sec. 4.4, App. A.6, Tables 2, 8). All 29 GSM8K problems that RL fixed relative to greedy base decoding were already solvable by the base under sampling: base pass@1 0.235, pass@4 0.60, pass@64 1.00. Steering the amplified features carries the format and extractability part of the gain, not a reasoning gain (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 3, App. A.5 Table 7).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B base and its GRPO checkpoints on GSM8K. Small n (29 problems).

### 2504.13837 — Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?, 2025
- Finding: RLVR raises pass@1 but not coverage at large k, and the base solves almost everything RL solves. Across six RLVR algorithms on MATH500, base pass@1 is 34.5 and base pass@256 is 96.2. RL pass@1 ranges 73.5-75.6 (GRPO 74.4, PPO 75.2, RLOO 75.0, DAPO 75.6) and RL pass@256 96.4-97.4 (App. C.5, Table 3). Per-problem coverage on MATH500 at 128 samples: solved by both 92.4%, base only 3.6%, RL only 1.0% (all solved by the base within 1,024 samples), neither 3.0%. On AIME24 at 1,024 samples: base only 13.3%, RL only 0.0% (Sec. 4.1, Table 2, App. C.7).
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-7B base vs SimpleRLZoo GRPO and six algorithms trained on 2,000 Omni-MATH problems. T 0.6, top-p 0.95, 16,384-token cap.

### 2610.24663 — What RL Brings to Reasoning: A Few Decisions the Base Model Would Not Make, 2026
- Finding: The RL gain sits at a few positions. "rl-replace" samples from the base and emits the RL model's greedy token only where the two greedy tokens differ (about 4% of positions). It recovers 90.5% [77.0, 108.6] of the AIME pass@1 gain (18.44 vs RL 19.32) and 106.6% on MATH-500 (85.25 vs 84.37) (Sec. 3.3, Table 2). Base pass@4 already equals RL pass@1 on AIME (20.79 vs 19.77), and the two meet at k=256 (61.64 vs 61.40) (Sec. 3.6, Table 6).
- Where measured: Qwen3-4B-Base vs its GRPO model. 120 AIME 2022-2025 problems and MATH-500. T 0.6, top-p 0.95, 8,192-token cap, 512 samples per problem.

### 2506.10947 — Spurious Rewards: Rethinking Training Signals in RLVR, 2025
- Finding: On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, most of the MATH-500 gain from RLVR arrives with rewards that carry no correctness signal: +29.1 points with ground truth, +24.1 with incorrect labels, +21.4 with random reward (p=0.5) over 49.4% (Sec. 2.2, Fig. 1). Random reward helps only when GRPO clipping is present. Clipping biases updates toward tokens already likely under the policy, and the authors call this a conjecture (Sec. 4, Fig. 4). OLMo-2-7B and Llama3 models gain mainly with ground-truth reward and stay flat or lose with spurious rewards (Sec. 3, Fig. 3).
- Where measured: GRPO without KL or entropy loss, lr 5e-7, 16 rollouts, 300 steps, DeepScaleR prompts. MATH-500 pass@1 at T=0. Qwen2.5(-Math) 1.5B/7B, OLMo-2-1124-7B (base 9.0%), Llama3.1-8B, Llama3.2-3B.

### 2504.20571 — Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning in Large Language Models with One Training Example, 2025
- Finding: RLVR on a single example the base can already often solve gives almost the full-dataset gain: MATH500 36.0 → 72.8 (π1) and 73.6 (π13). The 1.2k DSR-sub set gives 73.6 and the 7.5k MATH set 74.4. A format-only reward on 1,209 examples gives 65.0, so about 8.6 points of the single-example gain go beyond format correction (Table in Sec. 3). Test accuracy keeps rising after training accuracy on the example saturates ("post-saturation generalization"). Self-reflection frequency rises, and an entropy-loss term matters.
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with GRPO (also PPO). Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama3.2-3B-Instruct and R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B as replications. Six math benchmarks (MATH500, AIME24/25, AMC23, Minerva, OlympiadBench).

### 2503.01307 — Cognitive Behaviors that Enable Self-Improving Reasoners, or, Four Habits of Highly Effective STaRs, 2025
- Finding: Whether RL improves a model depends on whether the starting policy already shows verification and backtracking. Under identical RL on Countdown, Qwen-2.5-3B reaches about 60% and Llama-3.2-3B about 30%. Qwen shifts around step 30 to longer responses and higher accuracy (Sec. 2, Fig. 1). Priming Llama with traces that contain backtracking lets it match Qwen. Priming with incorrect solutions that show the behaviours matches priming with correct ones (Fig. 6). Empty or placeholder chains of the same length stay at about 30-35% (Fig. 5). RL amplifies verification and backtracking and suppresses backward chaining and subgoal setting (Fig. 3).
- Where measured: Qwen-2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-70B on Countdown. Priming sets of 1,200 games generated by Claude-3.5-Sonnet. OpenWebMath continued pretraining for Llama.

### 2504.07086 — A Sober Look at Progress in Language Model Reasoning: Pitfalls and Paths to Reproducibility, 2025
- Finding: Math-benchmark scores move with seed, hardware and evaluation stack. Pass@1 standard deviation across 20 seeds is 5-15 points, worst on AIME'24 (up to 15) and AMC'23 (up to 13). One question shifts AIME'24 by 3.3 points (Sec. 3, Fig. 2). The same model on five clusters varied by up to 8 points (OpenRS-1.5B) and 6 points (R1-Distill-7B) on AIME'24 (Fig. 10). Consecutive runs on identical hardware and stack gave 19.3-23.0% for Bespoke-Stratos-7B. Changing the evaluation framework alone gives 1-2 points (Table 2). Under a standardised re-evaluation, most RL methods give modest gains and SFT generalises more consistently.
- Where measured: Nine 1.5B-7B reasoning models on AIME'24, AMC'23 and MATH500. vLLM, lighteval vs evalchemy, A100 vs H100 clusters, CUDA determinism enforced in one stage.

### 2510.14773 — Finding Answers in Thought Matters: Revisiting Evaluation on Large Language Models with Reasoning, 2025
- Finding: Reasoning-model scores and model rankings depend on the answer-extraction rule. On MMLU-Pro, Qwen3-32B scores 15.3 under strict-match, 47.2 flexible-extract, 68.4 answer-is-correct, 66.8 last-extract and 77.0 with Answer Regeneration (Table 3). Answer Regeneration re-prompts with the model's output followed by "Answer:". It adds 1.2-5.0 points over each model's best rule on MMLU (Table 2) and is barely affected by which model regenerates (Table 7). On GSM8K disagreements read by humans, regeneration is correct in 16.3% and the rule in 6.1%. Incomplete thinking (2.8-6.8% of outputs) is graded correct at 25-42% (Table 1).
- Where measured: Qwen3-8B/14B/32B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B, R1-Qwen3-8B. MMLU, MMLU-Pro, GSM8K. lm-evaluation-harness extraction rules.

### 2310.01798 — Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet, 2023
- Finding: Without oracle labels, asking a model to review and revise its own answer lowers reasoning accuracy. GSM8K: GPT-3.5 75.9 → 75.1 → 74.7, GPT-4 95.5 → 91.5 → 89.0, Llama-2 62.0 → 43.5 → 36.5 over two self-correct rounds (Tables 3-4). GPT-3.5 keeps its initial GSM8K answer 74.7% of the time. Among changed answers, correct → incorrect is more common than incorrect → correct (Sec. 3, Fig. 1). Gains reported earlier came from oracle labels deciding when to stop.
- Where measured: GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4-Turbo, Llama-2 on GSM8K, CommonSenseQA and HotpotQA. 3-5 model calls per item.

### 2505.21067 — Why Distillation can Outperform Zero-RL: The Role of Flexible Reasoning, 2025
- Finding: A base model's measured math accuracy depends strongly on the prompt template. Qwen2.5-32B-Base MATH500 Avg@8 is 70.1 with no template, 46.8 with the Qwen-boxed template and 41.7 with the Qwen2.5-math-cot template. AIME2024 Avg@32 is 16.8 / 4.7 / 5.8 (App. C.2, Table 15). The paper reports the same direction for Qwen2.5-Math base models.
- Where measured: Qwen2.5-32B-Base. T 1, top-p 0.95, 32,768-token cap, official Qwen2.5-Math evaluation code.

### 2610.12056 — Structure Tax: How Structured Output affects LLMs Performance, 2026
- Finding: Making a model commit to the answer field before its reasoning collapses multi-step arithmetic even though reasoning length stays the same. GSM8K under XML (answer, reasoning) falls to 10.98% for Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma3-4B, against 83-87% under (reasoning, answer). On GSM8K, accuracy drops from 0.92 (R,A) to 0.55 for JSON (A,R) at a flat 108-127 words of reasoning. Reasoning-first formats win the best-format cell in 13 of 20 model-dataset pairs and also lower run-to-run variance. Smaller models collapse most (results, "Reasoning-first formats dominate" and "premature commitment").
- Where measured: Five models including Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma3-4B and Claude-Sonnet-4.5. GSM8K, MMLU, LogicBench, Spider. Freeform, JSON and XML outputs in both field orders.

### 2109.01903 — Robust fine-tuning of zero-shot models, 2021
- Finding: Linear interpolation (1-α)θ_0 + αθ_1 between a pretrained zero-shot model and its fine-tuned version (WiSE-FT) keeps or raises target accuracy at intermediate α. Accuracy stays high along the whole linear path, which the authors attribute to a shared part of the optimization trajectory (Sec. 5.2). End-to-end WiSE-FT at α=0.5 improves accuracy under distribution shift by 4-6 points over prior work and ImageNet accuracy by 1.6 points (Sec. 3, Table 1). The best fixed α is only 0-0.4 points better than α=0.5 on average (App. B, Table 3).
- Where measured: CLIP ViT models (plus 12 more backbones, App. C) fine-tuned on ImageNet, evaluated on ImageNetV2, -R, Sketch, ObjectNet and -A, plus six further shifts and seven transfer datasets.

### 1709.06560 — Deep Reinforcement Learning that Matters, 2017
- Finding: Differences between groups of runs that vary only in random seed can be statistically significant. Ten TRPO runs with identical hyperparameters, split into two groups of five seeds, give learning curves from different distributions on HalfCheetah: 2-sample t = -9.0916, p = 0.0016 (Sec. "Random Seeds", Fig. 5). Comparing algorithms needs many seeds and significance tests. ACKTR vs DDPG on Walker2d is not significant: t = 1.03, p = 0.334, bootstrapped difference 44.47% (-80.62%, 111.72%).
- Where measured: TRPO, DDPG, PPO, ACKTR on MuJoCo continuous-control tasks (HalfCheetah-v1, Hopper, Walker2d), 10 trials per configuration.
